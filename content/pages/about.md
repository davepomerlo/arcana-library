# About this archive

The Arcana Library is an open archive of the world's sacred and ancient texts, philosophy and metaphysics, the esoteric traditions, and historical accounts of the paranormal. Wherever possible it shows the **original language beside the translation**, and every page says **where its text came from and how far it has been checked**.

It is inspired by the breadth of sites like Biblioteca Pleyades, but it works differently in three ways:

1. **Primary sources first.** The archive prefers the texts themselves over articles about them, and the earliest accounts over later retellings.
2. **Clear provenance.** Each page names its source edition, the kind of translation used, and how far the original has been checked.
3. **No copied copyright material.** The archive does not republish modern articles or translations without permission. Texts here are public domain, newly translated for the archive, or written for it, and everything else is *linked* in the Library.

## What the labels mean

**Translation type**

- **AI-assisted translation.** A new translation made for this archive with the help of an AI model, then edited. Every AI-assisted page shows the original beside it and links at least one published scholarly translation for comparison. These translations aim to be close and readable. They are not a substitute for a critical scholarly translation.
- **Public-domain translation.** A published translation whose copyright has expired, with the translator and date named.
- **Openly licensed translation.** Used under the licence stated on the page.
- **Research article (case file).** Written for the archive from cited primary sources. Paranormal case files set out what the sources say, where they disagree, what has been proposed, and an assessment. They neither debunk nor promote.

**Checking status** (how far the *original-language* text has been compared with a published edition)

- **✓ checked.** Compared line by line with the cited edition.
- **≈ partly checked.** Some sections compared. The page says which.
- **○ unchecked.** Typed from a standard edition but not yet proofread against it. Corrections are very welcome.

## What counts as a reliable source

A source is preferred if it is:

- an **original text in a critical or standard edition** (Taishō, Diels–Kranz, Masoretic Text, Oxford Classical Texts, Zhengtong Daozang, and so on);
- published by a **university project, national library or learned society** (the Chinese Text Project, ETCSL, eBL, GRETIL, SuttaCentral, Sefaria, the Perseus Digital Library, the National Diet Library, Nichibunken, the Society for Psychical Research, CNES/GEIPAN);
- the work of a **long-running independent scholar** who cites manuscripts and editions (for example Joseph H. Peterson's Esoteric Archives or Adam McLean's Alchemy Web Site);
- for anomalies, a **contemporary or near-contemporary account**, ideally with named witnesses, and later **instrumented or statistical study** where one exists.

Sources that make claims without evidence, or that cannot be traced, are not used as evidence. They may be described as part of a story's reception.

## How to help

- Spot a typo in an original text, or a mistranslation? Every page has an **Edit this page on GitHub** link.
- Want to add a text? See the contributor guide (CONTRIBUTING.md) in the repository. Adding a text means writing one Markdown file.
- Checking an unchecked original against its edition is the single most useful thing a reader can do.
