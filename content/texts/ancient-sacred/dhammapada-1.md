::: note Key words
***dhamma*** has many senses. In verses 1–2 it most likely means “mental states” or “experiences”, all of which are led by mind. In verse 5 it means “law” or “principle”. ***mano*** is “mind”; ***vera*** is enmity or hatred; ***Māra*** is the tempter, the personified power of death and desire.
:::

::: parallel 1
Manopubbaṅgamā dhammā, manoseṭṭhā manomayā;
Manasā ce paduṭṭhena, bhāsati vā karoti vā;
Tato naṃ dukkhamanveti, cakkaṃva vahato padaṃ.
---
Mind goes before all things; mind is their chief, they are made by mind.
If one speaks or acts with a corrupted mind,
suffering follows, as the wheel follows the foot of the ox.
:::

::: parallel 2
Manopubbaṅgamā dhammā, manoseṭṭhā manomayā;
Manasā ce pasannena, bhāsati vā karoti vā;
Tato naṃ sukhamanveti, chāyāva anapāyinī.
---
Mind goes before all things; mind is their chief, they are made by mind.
If one speaks or acts with a clear and confident mind,
happiness follows, like a shadow that never leaves.
:::

::: parallel 3
Akkocchi maṃ avadhi maṃ, ajini maṃ ahāsi me;
Ye ca taṃ upanayhanti, veraṃ tesaṃ na sammati.
---
“He abused me, he struck me, he beat me, he robbed me”:
in those who nurse such thoughts, hatred is not appeased.
:::

::: parallel 4
Akkocchi maṃ avadhi maṃ, ajini maṃ ahāsi me;
Ye ca taṃ nupanayhanti, veraṃ tesūpasammati.
---
“He abused me, he struck me, he beat me, he robbed me”:
in those who do not nurse such thoughts, hatred is appeased.
:::

::: parallel 5
Na hi verena verāni, sammantīdha kudācanaṃ;
Averena ca sammanti, esa dhammo sanantano.
---
Hatred is never appeased by hatred in this world.
By non-hatred it is appeased: this is an eternal law.
:::

::: parallel 6
Pare ca na vijānanti, mayamettha yamāmase;
Ye ca tattha vijānanti, tato sammanti medhagā.
---
Others do not understand that we must all come to an end here.
Those who do understand this, their quarrels are settled.
:::

::: parallel 7
Subhānupassiṃ viharantaṃ, indriyesu asaṃvutaṃ;
Bhojanamhi cāmattaññuṃ, kusītaṃ hīnavīriyaṃ;
Taṃ ve pasahati māro, vāto rukkhaṃva dubbalaṃ.
---
One who lives looking for the pleasant, unguarded in the senses,
immoderate in food, idle and lacking energy:
Māra overpowers him, as the wind blows down a weak tree.
:::

::: parallel 8
Asubhānupassiṃ viharantaṃ, indriyesu susaṃvutaṃ;
Bhojanamhi ca mattaññuṃ, saddhaṃ āraddhavīriyaṃ;
Taṃ ve nappasahati māro, vāto selaṃva pabbataṃ.
---
One who lives contemplating the unattractive, well guarded in the senses,
moderate in food, full of faith and energy:
Māra cannot overpower him, as the wind cannot shake a mountain of rock.
:::

::: parallel 9
Anikkasāvo kāsāvaṃ, yo vatthaṃ paridahissati;
Apeto damasaccena, na so kāsāvamarahati.
---
Whoever puts on the ochre robe while still stained,
lacking self-control and truthfulness, is not worthy of the ochre robe.
:::

::: parallel 10
Yo ca vantakasāvassa, sīlesu susamāhito;
Upeto damasaccena, sa ve kāsāvamarahati.
---
But whoever has vomited out the stains, well settled in virtue,
possessed of self-control and truthfulness, is truly worthy of the ochre robe.
:::

::: parallel 11
Asāre sāramatino, sāre cāsāradassino;
Te sāraṃ nādhigacchanti, micchāsaṅkappagocarā.
---
Those who take the inessential for the essential and see the essential as inessential
do not reach the essential, roaming in the field of wrong intention.
:::

::: parallel 12
Sārañca sārato ñatvā, asārañca asārato;
Te sāraṃ adhigacchanti, sammāsaṅkappagocarā.
---
Those who know the essential as essential and the inessential as inessential
reach the essential, roaming in the field of right intention.
:::

::: parallel 13
Yathā agāraṃ ducchannaṃ, vuṭṭhī samativijjhati;
Evaṃ abhāvitaṃ cittaṃ, rāgo samativijjhati.
---
As rain pours into a badly thatched house,
so passion pours into an undeveloped mind.
:::

::: parallel 14
Yathā agāraṃ suchannaṃ, vuṭṭhī na samativijjhati;
Evaṃ subhāvitaṃ cittaṃ, rāgo na samativijjhati.
---
As rain does not pour into a well-thatched house,
so passion does not pour into a well-developed mind.
:::

::: parallel 15
Idha socati pecca socati, pāpakārī ubhayattha socati;
So socati so vihaññati, disvā kammakiliṭṭhamattano.
---
He grieves here, he grieves hereafter; the wrongdoer grieves in both worlds.
He grieves and is afflicted, seeing the corruption of his own deeds.
:::

::: parallel 16
Idha modati pecca modati, katapuñño ubhayattha modati;
So modati so pamodati, disvā kammavisuddhimattano.
---
He rejoices here, he rejoices hereafter; the doer of good rejoices in both worlds.
He rejoices and delights, seeing the purity of his own deeds.
:::

::: parallel 17
Idha tappati pecca tappati, pāpakārī ubhayattha tappati;
“Pāpaṃ me kata”nti tappati, bhiyyo tappati duggatiṃ gato.
---
He is tormented here, tormented hereafter; the wrongdoer is tormented in both worlds.
“I have done wrong”: so he is tormented, and more so when he has gone to a bad destination.
:::

::: parallel 18
Idha nandati pecca nandati, katapuñño ubhayattha nandati;
“Puññaṃ me kata”nti nandati, bhiyyo nandati suggatiṃ gato.
---
He is glad here, glad hereafter; the doer of good is glad in both worlds.
“I have done good”: so he is glad, and more so when he has gone to a good destination.
:::

::: parallel 19
Bahumpi ce saṃhita bhāsamāno, na takkaro hoti naro pamatto;
Gopova gāvo gaṇayaṃ paresaṃ, na bhāgavā sāmaññassa hoti.
---
Though he recites much scripture, the careless man who does not act on it
is like a cowherd counting other people's cows: he has no share in the holy life.
:::

::: parallel 20
Appampi ce saṃhita bhāsamāno, dhammassa hoti anudhammacārī;
Rāgañca dosañca pahāya mohaṃ, sammappajāno suvimuttacitto;
Anupādiyāno idha vā huraṃ vā, sa bhāgavā sāmaññassa hoti.
---
Though he recites little scripture, if he lives according to the teaching,
abandoning passion, hatred and delusion, knowing rightly, his mind well freed,
clinging to nothing here or hereafter, he has a share in the holy life.
:::
