::: note About this text
*Hávamál*, "the sayings of the High One", survives only in the Codex Regius, the Icelandic manuscript of about 1270 that preserves most of the Poetic Edda. Its last part is spoken by Odin himself. Stanzas 138 and following, often called the *Rúnatal*, "the tally of runes", tell how he won the runes by a self-sacrifice that many readers have compared to shamanic initiation and to the Crucifixion.

The tree is not named, but it is generally taken to be Yggdrasil, whose name may mean "Odin's horse" (*Yggr* is one of Odin's names), that is, the gallows.

Stanza numbers differ between editions. Bellows (1923) counts these as 139–142; most modern editions, following Neckel and Kuhn, count them as 138–141.
:::

::: parallel 138
Veit ek, at ek hekk vindga meiði á
nætr allar níu,
geiri undaðr ok gefinn Óðni,
sjalfr sjalfum mér,
á þeim meiði, er manngi veit
hvers af rótum renn.
---
I know that I hung on the windswept tree
all of nine nights,
wounded by a spear and given to Odin,
myself to myself,
on that tree of which no one knows
from what roots it runs.
:::

::: parallel 139
Við hleifi mik sældu
né við hornigi;
nýsta ek niðr,
nam ek upp rúnar,
æpandi nam,
fell ek aftr þaðan.
---
They gave me no comfort of bread,
nor of any horn to drink;
I peered down,
I took up the runes,
screaming I took them,
and I fell back from there.
:::

::: parallel 140
Fimbulljóð níu
nam ek af inum frægja syni
Bölþorns, Bestlu föður,
ok ek drykk of gat
ins dýra mjaðar,
ausinn Óðreri.
---
Nine mighty songs
I learned from the famous son
of Bölþorn, the father of Bestla,
and I got a drink
of the precious mead,
poured from Óðrerir.
:::

::: parallel 141
Þá nam ek frævask
ok fróðr vera
ok vaxa ok vel hafask;
orð mér af orði
orðs leitaði,
verk mér af verki
verks leitaði.
---
Then I began to quicken
and to be wise,
to grow and to thrive;
word led me on from word
to word,
deed led me on from deed
to deed.
:::

::: note Textual notes
- **138.** The manuscript reads *hvers hann af rótom renn*, "from what roots it runs". Many normalised texts, including the one followed here, drop *hann* for the metre.
- **139.** *sældu* is normalised from the manuscript's *seldo*. Editors disagree whether it means "they refreshed" or "they gave"; the sense is the same: Odin went without food and drink.
- **140.** The "famous son of Bölþorn" is Odin's maternal uncle, the brother of his mother Bestla. Óðrerir is the vessel, or the mead itself, of the mead of poetry.
- **141.** The verb *frævask* is related to *frjó*, "seed": "to become fertile, to sprout". The stanza describes knowledge that grows by itself once begun.
:::
