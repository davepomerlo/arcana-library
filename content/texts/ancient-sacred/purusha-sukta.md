::: note About this text
The *Puruṣa Sūkta* (Rigveda 10.90) imagines the universe as a giant Person (*puruṣa*) with a thousand heads, eyes and feet, who covers the earth and extends beyond it. The gods perform a sacrifice with this Person as the offering, and the parts of his body become the parts of the world. Dismemberment myths of this kind appear in many traditions: the Norse giant Ymir, whose skull becomes the sky, and the Chinese Pangu, whose eyes become the sun and moon.

Verse 12 is the earliest statement of the four *varṇa* (social classes): priest from the mouth, warrior from the arms, commoner from the thighs, servant from the feet. Most scholars, including Jamison and Brereton, regard the hymn as one of the latest in the Rigveda, and the class verse as a reflection of a society just taking that shape. The hymn has been central to later debates about caste, including B. R. Ambedkar's.
:::

## The thousand-headed Person

::: parallel 1
सहस्रशीर्षा पुरुषः सहस्राक्षः सहस्रपात् ।
स भूमिं विश्वतो वृत्वात्यतिष्ठद्दशाङ्गुलम् ॥
---
sahasraśīrṣā puruṣaḥ sahasrākṣaḥ sahasrapāt |
sa bhūmiṃ viśvato vṛtvāty atiṣṭhad daśāṅgulam ||
---
The Person has a thousand heads, a thousand eyes, a thousand feet.
He covered the earth on every side and stood out beyond it by ten fingers' breadth.
:::

::: parallel 2
पुरुष एवेदं सर्वं यद्भूतं यच्च भव्यम् ।
उतामृतत्वस्येशानो यदन्नेनातिरोहति ॥
---
puruṣa evedaṃ sarvaṃ yad bhūtaṃ yac ca bhavyam |
utāmṛtatvasyeśāno yad annenātirohati ||
---
The Person is all this, whatever has been and whatever is to be.
He is lord of immortality, and of what grows beyond it through food.
:::

::: parallel 3
एतावानस्य महिमातो ज्यायाँश्च पूरुषः ।
पादोऽस्य विश्वा भूतानि त्रिपादस्यामृतं दिवि ॥
---
etāvān asya mahimāto jyāyāṃś ca pūruṣaḥ |
pādo 'sya viśvā bhūtāni tripād asyāmṛtaṃ divi ||
---
So great is his greatness, and the Person is greater still:
all beings are a quarter of him; three quarters of him are the immortal in heaven.
:::

::: parallel 4
त्रिपादूर्ध्व उदैत्पुरुषः पादोऽस्येहाभवत्पुनः ।
ततो विष्वङ् व्यक्रामत्साशनानशने अभि ॥
---
tripād ūrdhva ud ait puruṣaḥ pādo 'syehābhavat punaḥ |
tato viṣvaṅ vy akrāmat sāśanānaśane abhi ||
---
With three quarters the Person rose upward; one quarter of him came into being here again.
From there he spread out in every direction, over what eats and what does not eat.
:::

## The world from his body

::: parallel 5
ब्राह्मणोऽस्य मुखमासीद्बाहू राजन्यः कृतः ।
ऊरू तदस्य यद्वैश्यः पद्भ्यां शूद्रो अजायत ॥
---
brāhmaṇo 'sya mukham āsīd bāhū rājanyaḥ kṛtaḥ |
ūrū tad asya yad vaiśyaḥ padbhyāṃ śūdro ajāyata ||
---
(12) The priest was his mouth; the warrior was made his arms;
his thighs became the commoner; from his feet the servant was born.
:::

::: parallel 6
चन्द्रमा मनसो जातश्चक्षोः सूर्यो अजायत ।
मुखादिन्द्रश्चाग्निश्च प्राणाद्वायुरजायत ॥
---
candramā manaso jātaś cakṣoḥ sūryo ajāyata |
mukhād indraś cāgniś ca prāṇād vāyur ajāyata ||
---
(13) The moon was born from his mind; from his eye the sun was born;
from his mouth Indra and Agni; from his breath the wind was born.
:::

::: parallel 7
नाभ्या आसीदन्तरिक्षं शीर्ष्णो द्यौः समवर्तत ।
पद्भ्यां भूमिर्दिशः श्रोत्रात्तथा लोकाँ अकल्पयन् ॥
---
nābhyā āsīd antarikṣaṃ śīrṣṇo dyauḥ sam avartata |
padbhyāṃ bhūmir diśaḥ śrotrāt tathā lokāṃ akalpayan ||
---
(14) From his navel came the middle air; from his head the sky came forth;
from his feet the earth, from his ear the directions. So they shaped the worlds.
:::

::: note Words worth knowing
- **पुरुष *puruṣa***: "person, man". In later Sāṃkhya philosophy and in the Yoga Sūtras the same word means pure consciousness, the witness distinct from matter (see the archive's [Yoga Sūtras](yoga-sutras-selections.html)).
- **दशाङ्गुलम् *daśāṅgulam***, "ten fingers": the Person exceeds the world by a small measure, a way of saying he is greater than all that exists.
- **पाद *pāda***: "foot", and also "quarter", as a quadruped's foot is a quarter of its legs. The play on words gives the image of a world that is only one quarter of the divine.
:::
