::: note Notice: why this text, and how it was checked
No Sumerian text tells the story of creation from start to finish. What survives are short cosmological prologues at the start of poems about other things, and this one, from "Gilgamesh, Enkidu and the Netherworld", is the fullest. Its Sumerian is known from clay tablets copied by scribes around 1800–1700 BCE; no single tablet is complete, so the text below is a **composite** reconstructed by modern scholars from many manuscripts. Readings of individual signs can change as new tablets are published. The transliteration here follows the University of Oxford's ETCSL edition exactly; the translation is our own.
:::

::: note About this text
The poem was known to the ancients by its first words, *ud re-a*, "in those days". After this prologue it tells how a tree washed down the Euphrates is planted by the goddess Inana, how Gilgamesh cuts it down and makes a ball and stick from its wood, and how his servant Enkidu goes down to the netherworld to fetch them and does not return. Its second half was translated into Akkadian and attached to the Babylonian *Epic of Gilgamesh* as Tablet XII.

The prologue looks back to the moment when the universe took its present shape: heaven and earth parted, and the great gods divided the world between them.
:::

## In the distant days

::: parallel 1
ud re-a ud su₃-ra₂ re-a
ĝi₆ re-a ĝi₆ ba₉-ra₂ re-a
mu re-a mu su₃-ra₂ re-a
---
In those days, in those far-off days;
in those nights, in those far-away nights;
in those years, in those far-off years:
:::

::: parallel 2
ud ul niĝ₂-du₇-e pa ed₂-a-ba
ud ul niĝ₂-du₇-e mi₂ zid dug₄-ga-a-ba
eš₃ kalam-ma-ka ninda šu₂-a-ba
{im}šu-rin-na kalam-ma-ka niĝ₂-tab ak-a-ba
---
in days of old, when what was needed had been made to appear;
in days of old, when what was needed had first been properly cared for;
when bread had been eaten in the shrines of the Land,
when the ovens of the Land had been fired:
:::

## Heaven and earth divided

::: parallel 3
an ki-ta ba-da-ba₉-ra₂-a-ba
ki an-ta ba-da-sur-ra-a-ba
mu nam-lu₂-u₁₈-lu ba-an-ĝar-ra-a-ba
---
when heaven had been parted from earth,
when earth had been marked off from heaven,
when the name of humankind had been established:
:::

::: parallel 4
ud an-ne₂ an ba-an-de₆-a-ba
{d}en-lil₂-le ki ba-an-de₆-a-ba
{d}ereš-ki-gal-la-ra kur-ra saĝ rig₇-bi-še₃ im-ma-ab-rig₇-a-ba
---
when An had carried off the heavens,
when Enlil had carried off the earth,
when the netherworld had been handed to Ereshkigal as her gift:
:::

::: note Words worth knowing
- **an** and **ki**: "heaven" and "earth". Written together, *an-ki* is the Sumerian word for the universe. **An** is also the name of the sky god, and **Enlil** ("Lord Wind") the god of the air between, who rules the earth.
- **ba₉-ra₂**, "to part, separate": the same image as the Egyptian god Shu holding the sky up from the earth, the Māori Tāne pushing Rangi from Papa, and the Japanese and Chinese accounts in which the clear rises and the heavy sinks.
- **kur**: "mountain", "foreign land" and "netherworld". **Ereshkigal**, "Lady of the Great Earth", is queen of the dead.
- **ninda** (bread) and **{im}šu-rin-na** (the clay oven): civilisation begins with baking. For the Sumerians, the cosmos was not complete until there was bread in the temples.
:::
