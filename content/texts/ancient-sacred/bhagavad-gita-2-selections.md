::: note About this text
The Bhagavad Gītā, "the song of the Lord", is a dialogue of 700 verses set just before the great battle of the Mahābhārata. The warrior Arjuna, seeing his teachers and kinsmen in the opposing army, refuses to fight. His charioteer Krishna, who turns out to be God, answers him. Chapter 2 gives the first answer: the true self (*ātman*, here called *dehin*, "the embodied one") cannot kill or be killed.

Verse 2.20 closely echoes Kaṭha Upaniṣad 1.2.18, one sign of how the Gītā draws on the older Upaniṣads.
:::

::: parallel 2.19
य एनं वेत्ति हन्तारं यश्चैनं मन्यते हतम् ।
उभौ तौ न विजानीतो नायं हन्ति न हन्यते ॥
---
ya enaṃ vetti hantāraṃ yaś cainaṃ manyate hatam |
ubhau tau na vijānīto nāyaṃ hanti na hanyate ||
---
Whoever thinks this one a killer, and whoever thinks it killed,
neither of them understands: it does not kill, and it is not killed.
:::

::: parallel 2.20
न जायते म्रियते वा कदाचिन् नायं भूत्वा भविता वा न भूयः ।
अजो नित्यः शाश्वतोऽयं पुराणो न हन्यते हन्यमाने शरीरे ॥
---
na jāyate mriyate vā kadācin nāyaṃ bhūtvā bhavitā vā na bhūyaḥ |
ajo nityaḥ śāśvato 'yaṃ purāṇo na hanyate hanyamāne śarīre ||
---
It is never born, and it never dies; it did not come to be, and it will not cease to be.
Unborn, eternal, everlasting, ancient, it is not killed when the body is killed.
:::

::: parallel 2.22
वासांसि जीर्णानि यथा विहाय नवानि गृह्णाति नरोऽपराणि ।
तथा शरीराणि विहाय जीर्णान्यन्यानि संयाति नवानि देही ॥
---
vāsāṃsi jīrṇāni yathā vihāya navāni gṛhṇāti naro 'parāṇi |
tathā śarīrāṇi vihāya jīrṇāny anyāni saṃyāti navāni dehī ||
---
As a man throws off worn-out clothes and puts on others that are new,
so the embodied one throws off worn-out bodies and passes into others that are new.
:::

::: parallel 2.23
नैनं छिन्दन्ति शस्त्राणि नैनं दहति पावकः ।
न चैनं क्लेदयन्त्यापो न शोषयति मारुतः ॥
---
nainaṃ chindanti śastrāṇi nainaṃ dahati pāvakaḥ |
na cainaṃ kledayanty āpo na śoṣayati mārutaḥ ||
---
Weapons do not cut it, fire does not burn it,
water does not wet it, and the wind does not dry it.
:::

::: parallel 2.47
कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन ।
मा कर्मफलहेतुर्भूर्मा ते सङ्गोऽस्त्वकर्मणि ॥
---
karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana |
mā karmaphalahetur bhūr mā te saṅgo 'stv akarmaṇi ||
---
Your right is to the action alone, never to its fruits.
Do not let the fruit of action be your motive, and do not cling to inaction either.
:::

::: note On verse 2.47
This verse states the Gītā's idea of *karma-yoga*, the discipline of action. Arjuna is not told to give up acting, which is impossible, but to give up his claim on the results. The word *adhikāra*, "right" or "entitlement", comes from the language of ritual: it names who is qualified to perform a rite. Krishna applies it to every action.
:::
