::: note About this text
The *Kalevala* is not an ancient book but a 19th-century compilation from very old oral poetry. The physician and folklorist Elias Lönnrot travelled in Finnish and Russian Karelia collecting songs from runo singers such as Arhippa Perttunen, then arranged them into a continuous epic: a first version in 1835 and the longer, standard edition of 1849, with 50 poems. It became the founding text of Finnish national identity.

In the folk versions of the creation song, it is often the singer-hero Väinämöinen, floating on the sea, on whose knee the bird nests. Lönnrot gave the role to a new figure, the "Water-Mother" (*veen emonen*), the virgin of the air Ilmatar who has descended into the sea. A goldeneye or teal (*sotka*) looking for a place to nest finds her raised knee and lays six golden eggs and a seventh of iron. As the bird broods, the knee grows hot, and the Water-Mother jerks it.

The idea that the world was made from an egg appears from India (the golden embryo, *hiraṇyagarbha*) to China (Pangu) and Japan. See the archive's [Nihon shoki](nihon-shoki-opening.html).
:::

## The eggs break

::: parallel 1
Vavahutti polveansa,
järkytti jäseniänsä:
munat vierähti vetehen,
meren aaltohon ajaikse;
karskahti munat muruiksi,
katkieli kappaleiksi.
---
She shook her knee,
jerked her limbs:
the eggs rolled into the water,
slid into the waves of the sea;
the eggs cracked into crumbs,
broke into fragments.
:::

::: parallel 2
Ei munat mutahan joua,
siepalehet veen sekahan.
Muuttuivat murut hyviksi,
kappalehet kaunoisiksi:
---
The eggs were not lost in the mud,
nor the pieces in the water.
The crumbs turned into good things,
the pieces into beautiful ones:
:::

## The world from the egg

::: parallel 3
munasen alainen puoli
alaiseksi maaemäksi,
munasen yläinen puoli
yläiseksi taivahaksi;
---
the lower half of the egg
into the lower, mother earth,
the upper half of the egg
into the upper heaven;
:::

::: parallel 4
yläpuoli ruskeaista
päivöseksi paistamahan,
yläpuoli valkeaista,
se kuuksi kumottamahan;
mi munassa kirjavaista,
ne tähiksi taivahalle,
mi munassa mustukaista,
nepä ilman pilvilöiksi.
---
the upper part of the yolk
into the sun, to shine,
the upper part of the white,
that into the moon, to gleam;
whatever in the egg was speckled
into the stars of heaven,
whatever in the egg was dark
into the clouds of the air.
:::

::: note Words worth knowing
- ***maaemä***: "earth-mother", the earth as a mother. The world in this poem is the egg turned inside out, its lower shell becoming the ground under us and the upper shell the dome of the sky.
- ***ruskea*** here means the yolk (literally "the brown"), and ***valkea*** the white.
- **The metre**: every line has eight syllables, usually with two words beginning with the same sound (*munat … meren*, *kappalehet kaunoisiksi*), and most lines are followed by a second line that repeats the thought in other words. This "Kalevala metre" is shared with Estonian and Karelian song and is probably more than two thousand years old.
:::
