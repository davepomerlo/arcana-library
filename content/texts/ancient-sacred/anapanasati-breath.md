::: note About this text
In this discourse the Buddha teaches sixteen steps of mindfulness while breathing, in four groups of four: body, feelings, mind and phenomena. The same sixteen steps appear in many other discourses, including sixteen in the Saṃyutta Nikāya's chapter on the breath, and versions survive in Chinese and Sanskrit, which makes this one of the best-attested meditation instructions in early Buddhism.

The first step is not to control the breath but to know it. The key verb, *pajānāti*, means "he knows clearly". Only from the third step onward does the language change to *sikkhati*, "he trains": training to feel the whole body, and then to calm it.
:::

## Sitting down

::: parallel 0
Idha, bhikkhave, bhikkhu araññagato vā rukkhamūlagato vā suññāgāragato vā nisīdati pallaṅkaṃ ābhujitvā ujuṃ kāyaṃ paṇidhāya parimukhaṃ satiṃ upaṭṭhapetvā. So satova assasati, satova passasati.
---
Here, monks, a monk who has gone to the forest, or to the root of a tree, or to an empty hut, sits down, folds his legs crosswise, sets his body upright and establishes mindfulness in front of him. Mindful, he breathes in; mindful, he breathes out.
:::

## The first four steps: the body

::: parallel 1
Dīghaṃ vā assasanto "dīghaṃ assasāmī"ti pajānāti,
dīghaṃ vā passasanto "dīghaṃ passasāmī"ti pajānāti.
---
Breathing in long, he knows: "I breathe in long."
Breathing out long, he knows: "I breathe out long."
:::

::: parallel 2
Rassaṃ vā assasanto "rassaṃ assasāmī"ti pajānāti,
rassaṃ vā passasanto "rassaṃ passasāmī"ti pajānāti.
---
Breathing in short, he knows: "I breathe in short."
Breathing out short, he knows: "I breathe out short."
:::

::: parallel 3
"Sabbakāyapaṭisaṃvedī assasissāmī"ti sikkhati,
"sabbakāyapaṭisaṃvedī passasissāmī"ti sikkhati.
---
He trains: "Experiencing the whole body, I will breathe in."
He trains: "Experiencing the whole body, I will breathe out."
:::

::: parallel 4
"Passambhayaṃ kāyasaṅkhāraṃ assasissāmī"ti sikkhati,
"passambhayaṃ kāyasaṅkhāraṃ passasissāmī"ti sikkhati.
---
He trains: "Calming the bodily process, I will breathe in."
He trains: "Calming the bodily process, I will breathe out."
:::

::: note Words worth knowing
- **sati**: mindfulness, literally "remembering": keeping something in mind. *Parimukhaṃ*, "in front", may mean at the face or simply "to the fore".
- **assasati / passasati**: to breathe in / out. Even this is disputed: one commentarial tradition takes the two words the other way round. Ānandajoti follows the Sutta commentary, as does this page.
- **sabbakāya** "the whole body": commentators disagree whether this means the whole physical body or the whole "body" of the breath, from its beginning to its end. A Sanskrit parallel reads "the whole bodily process", supporting the breath reading.
- **kāyasaṅkhāra** "bodily process": in the early texts this is defined as the breath itself, so the fourth step calms the breath by knowing it.
:::

::: note Meditation and modern research
Whether practices like this one improve health is now studied in clinical trials. A 2014 meta-analysis from Johns Hopkins, covering 47 trials with active control groups, found moderate evidence that mindfulness programmes reduce anxiety, depression and pain, but no evidence that they work better than other active treatments such as medication or exercise. See the archive's guide to [breath and meditation research](breath-meditation-research.html).
:::
