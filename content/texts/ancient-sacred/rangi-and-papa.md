::: note Notice
Governor George Grey published this story under his own name, but much of it was written by the Te Arawa scholar **Wiremu Maihi Te Rangikāheke**, whom Grey did not acknowledge, and Grey combined, altered and cut the manuscripts he was given. Māori traditions also differ from iwi (tribe) to iwi: in some, Rangi and Papa are preceded by long genealogies of Te Kore (the Void) and Te Pō (the Night), and in some Papa was first the wife of Tangaroa. This is one important version, not *the* Māori creation story. We give two short passages that could be checked.
:::

## One pair of ancestors

::: parallel 1
Kotahi ano te tupuna o te tangata maori, ko Rangi-nui e tu nei, ko Papa-tua-nuku e takoto nei. Ki nga tangata maori, na Rangi raua ko Papa nga take o mua…
---
The Māori people have only one ancestry: great Rangi who stands above, and Papa-tū-ā-nuku who lies here below. For the Māori, Rangi and Papa are the origin of all things in the beginning…
:::

In Grey's telling, darkness lay over everything because the two still clung together, and their children, cramped between them, debated what to do. Tūmatauenga, father of war, wanted to kill their parents. Tāne-mahuta, father of the forests, disagreed:

## Tāne's counsel

::: parallel 2
Ka mea atu a Tane-mahuta … engari me wehewehe raua, ki runga tetahi, ki raro tetahi, kia kotahi hei tangata ke ki a tatou, kia kotahi hei matua ki a tatou. Ka whakaae ratou tahi.
---
Then Tāne-mahuta said: "… rather, let us separate them, one above and one below; let one be a stranger to us, and let one be a parent to us." And they all agreed together.
:::

The brothers tried one by one. Only Tāne succeeded, lying on his back with his head pressed against his mother and his feet against his father, and pushing until the sky rose far above. Light came into the world, and the people hidden between the two bodies were revealed. Only Tāwhirimātea, god of winds, had refused, and he still makes war on his brothers in storms. Rangi's tears for Papa fall as rain and dew, and her sighs rise to him as mist.

::: note Words worth knowing
- ***e tu nei*** / ***e takoto nei***: "who stands here" / "who lies here". The present tense matters: Rangi and Papa are not past beings but the sky and earth you can see.
- ***tangata ke***: a stranger, someone outside the family. ***matua***: parent. The sky becomes distant, while the earth remains a parent, a "nursing mother" in Grey's English.
- ***Papa-tū-ā-nuku***: usually written today **Papatūānuku**, the earth mother. Land is still spoken of as *whenua*, which also means the placenta.
:::
