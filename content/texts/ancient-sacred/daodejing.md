::: note Reading this translation
道 *dào* is left as **Dao**. 德 *dé* is “virtue” in the old sense of inner power or potency. 無為 *wúwéi* is “non-action”: acting without forcing, not doing nothing. 聖人 *shèngrén* is “the sage”. 萬物 *wànwù*, literally “ten thousand things”, means everything that exists.
:::

## Book One · The Dao (chapters 1–37)

::: parallel 1
道可道，非常道。
名可名，非常名。
無名天地之始；有名萬物之母。
故常無欲，以觀其妙；
常有欲，以觀其徼。
此兩者，同出而異名，同謂之玄。
玄之又玄，眾妙之門。
---
The Dao that can be told is not the constant Dao.
The name that can be named is not the constant name.
Nameless, it is the beginning of Heaven and Earth; named, it is the mother of the ten thousand things.
So, always without desire, one sees its subtlety;
always with desire, one sees its boundaries.
These two come out together but are named differently; together they are called the mystery.
Mystery upon mystery: the gate of all subtleties.
:::

::: parallel 2
天下皆知美之為美，斯惡已；
皆知善之為善，斯不善已。
故有無相生，難易相成，
長短相較，高下相傾，
音聲相和，前後相隨。
是以聖人處無為之事，行不言之教；
萬物作焉而不辭，生而不有，
為而不恃，功成而弗居。
夫唯弗居，是以不去。
---
When everyone knows beauty as beauty, there is already ugliness;
when everyone knows good as good, there is already the not-good.
So being and non-being give birth to each other, hard and easy complete each other,
long and short measure each other, high and low lean on each other,
note and voice harmonise with each other, before and after follow each other.
Therefore the sage abides in the work of non-action and practises the teaching without words.
The ten thousand things arise and he does not turn away; he produces but does not possess,
acts but does not rely on it, completes the work but does not dwell in it.
Only because he does not dwell in it, it does not leave him.
:::

::: parallel 3
不尚賢，使民不爭；
不貴難得之貨，使民不為盜；
不見可欲，使民心不亂。
是以聖人之治，虛其心，實其腹，弱其志，強其骨。
常使民無知無欲。使夫智者不敢為也。
為無為，則無不治。
---
Do not exalt the worthy, and the people will not compete.
Do not prize goods that are hard to get, and the people will not steal.
Do not display what can be desired, and the people's hearts will not be disturbed.
So the sage governs by emptying their minds and filling their bellies, softening their ambitions and strengthening their bones,
always keeping the people without cunning and without craving, so that the clever dare not act.
Act by non-action, and nothing is left ungoverned.
:::

::: parallel 4
道沖而用之或不盈。
淵兮似萬物之宗。
挫其銳，解其紛，和其光，同其塵。
湛兮似或存。
吾不知誰之子，象帝之先。
---
The Dao is empty, yet when used it is never filled.
How deep! It seems the ancestor of the ten thousand things.
It blunts the sharp, untangles the knots, softens the glare, becomes one with the dust.
How still! It seems perhaps to exist.
I do not know whose child it is. It seems to come before the Lord on High.
:::

::: parallel 5
天地不仁，以萬物為芻狗；
聖人不仁，以百姓為芻狗。
天地之間，其猶橐籥乎？
虛而不屈，動而愈出。
多言數窮，不如守中。
---
Heaven and Earth are not humane; they treat the ten thousand things as straw dogs.
The sage is not humane; he treats the hundred clans as straw dogs.
Is not the space between Heaven and Earth like a bellows?
Empty, yet never exhausted; the more it moves, the more comes out.
Much talk soon runs dry. Better to hold to the centre.
:::

::: parallel 6
谷神不死，是謂玄牝。
玄牝之門，是謂天地根。
綿綿若存，用之不勤。
---
The spirit of the valley never dies; it is called the mysterious female.
The gate of the mysterious female is called the root of Heaven and Earth.
Gossamer-fine, it seems to last; use it and it is never worn out.
:::

::: parallel 7
天長地久。
天地所以能長且久者，以其不自生，故能長生。
是以聖人後其身而身先；外其身而身存。
非以其無私邪？故能成其私。
---
Heaven is lasting and Earth endures.
They can last and endure because they do not live for themselves; so they can live long.
So the sage puts himself last and finds himself first, puts himself outside and finds himself preserved.
Is it not because he has no self-interest that he can fulfil himself?
:::

::: parallel 8
上善若水。
水善利萬物而不爭，處眾人之所惡，故幾於道。
居善地，心善淵，與善仁，
言善信，政善治，事善能，動善時。
夫唯不爭，故無尤。
---
The highest good is like water.
Water is good at benefiting the ten thousand things without contending. It settles where everyone dislikes to be, and so it is close to the Dao.
In dwelling, good is the ground; in the heart, good is depth; in giving, good is kindness;
in speech, good is trust; in governing, good is order; in work, good is ability; in moving, good is timing.
Only because it does not contend is it free of blame.
:::

::: parallel 9
持而盈之，不如其已；
揣而銳之，不可長保。
金玉滿堂，莫之能守；
富貴而驕，自遺其咎。
功遂身退，天之道。
---
Holding a cup and filling it to the brim is not as good as stopping in time.
Hammer a blade to its sharpest and it will not keep its edge long.
A hall full of gold and jade cannot be guarded.
Wealth and rank with arrogance bring their own ruin.
When the work is done, step back: this is the Way of Heaven.
:::

::: parallel 10
載營魄抱一，能無離乎？
專氣致柔，能如嬰兒乎？
滌除玄覽，能無疵乎？
愛國治民，能無為乎？
天門開闔，能為雌乎？
明白四達，能無知乎？
生之、畜之，生而不有，
為而不恃，長而不宰，是謂玄德。
---
Carrying body and soul and embracing the One, can you keep them from parting?
Concentrating the breath until it is supple, can you be like an infant?
Washing clean the dark mirror, can you leave it without a spot?
Loving the state and ordering the people, can you do it without action?
As the gates of Heaven open and close, can you play the female part?
Seeing clearly in all four directions, can you remain without knowledge?
Giving birth and rearing, giving birth without possessing,
acting without relying on it, leading without ruling: this is called mysterious virtue.
:::

::: parallel 11
三十輻共一轂，當其無，有車之用。
埏埴以為器，當其無，有器之用。
鑿戶牖以為室，當其無，有室之用。
故有之以為利，無之以為用。
---
Thirty spokes share one hub; in its emptiness lies the use of the cart.
Clay is shaped into a vessel; in its emptiness lies the use of the vessel.
Doors and windows are cut to make a room; in its emptiness lies the use of the room.
So what is there gives the benefit; what is not there gives the use.
:::

::: parallel 12
五色令人目盲；五音令人耳聾；五味令人口爽；
馳騁畋獵，令人心發狂；
難得之貨，令人行妨。
是以聖人為腹不為目，故去彼取此。
---
The five colours blind the eye; the five notes deafen the ear; the five flavours dull the palate.
Racing and hunting madden the heart.
Goods hard to get lead conduct astray.
So the sage attends to the belly, not the eye. He lets go of that and takes this.
:::

::: parallel 13
寵辱若驚，貴大患若身。
何謂寵辱若驚？寵為下，得之若驚，失之若驚，是謂寵辱若驚。
何謂貴大患若身？吾所以有大患者，為吾有身，及吾無身，吾有何患？
故貴以身為天下，若可寄天下；
愛以身為天下，若可託天下。
---
Favour and disgrace are alarming alike; treat great trouble as you treat your own body.
Why are favour and disgrace alarming alike? Favour is lowly: gaining it is alarming, losing it is alarming. That is why.
Why treat great trouble as your own body? I have great trouble because I have a body. If I had no body, what trouble would I have?
So one who values the world as his own body can be entrusted with the world;
one who loves the world as his own body can be given charge of the world.
:::

::: parallel 14
視之不見，名曰夷；聽之不聞，名曰希；搏之不得，名曰微。
此三者不可致詰，故混而為一。
其上不皦，其下不昧，繩繩不可名，復歸於無物。
是謂無狀之狀，無物之象，是謂惚恍。
迎之不見其首，隨之不見其後。
執古之道，以御今之有。
能知古始，是謂道紀。
---
Looked at but not seen, it is called the invisible. Listened to but not heard, it is called the inaudible. Grasped at but not held, it is called the intangible.
These three cannot be fully fathomed, so they merge and become one.
Its top is not bright; its bottom is not dark. Unbroken, unnameable, it returns again to no-thing.
This is called the shape without shape, the image without substance. It is called the elusive and evanescent.
Meet it and you do not see its head; follow it and you do not see its back.
Hold to the Dao of old to manage what exists today.
To know the ancient beginning: this is called the thread of the Dao.
:::

::: parallel 15
古之善為士者，微妙玄通，深不可識。
夫唯不可識，故強為之容：
豫兮若冬涉川；猶兮若畏四鄰；
儼兮其若客；渙兮若冰之將釋；
敦兮其若樸；曠兮其若谷；混兮其若濁。
孰能濁以靜之徐清？孰能安以久動之徐生？
保此道者，不欲盈。夫唯不盈，故能蔽不新成。
---
The good adepts of old were subtle, wondrous, mysterious, penetrating, too deep to be known.
Because they cannot be known, I can only force a description:
Hesitant, like one crossing a river in winter; wary, like one afraid of the neighbours on every side;
reserved, like a guest; yielding, like ice about to melt;
genuine, like uncarved wood; open, like a valley; murky, like muddy water.
Who can be muddy and, by stillness, slowly become clear? Who can be at rest and, by long movement, slowly come to life?
Whoever keeps this Dao does not want to be full. Because he is not full, he can wear out and not be renewed.
:::

::: parallel 16
致虛極，守靜篤。
萬物並作，吾以觀復。
夫物芸芸，各復歸其根。
歸根曰靜，是謂復命。
復命曰常，知常曰明。
不知常，妄作凶。
知常容，容乃公，公乃王，王乃天，天乃道，道乃久，沒身不殆。
---
Reach the utmost emptiness; hold fast to stillness.
The ten thousand things rise together, and I watch them return.
Things flourish and flourish, and each goes back to its root.
Going back to the root is called stillness; this is called returning to one's destiny.
Returning to destiny is called the constant; knowing the constant is called clarity.
Not knowing the constant, people act wildly and meet disaster.
Knowing the constant, one is all-embracing; all-embracing, impartial; impartial, kingly; kingly, heavenly; heavenly, one with the Dao; one with the Dao, lasting. To the end of life one is not in danger.
:::

::: parallel 17
太上，下知有之；
其次，親而譽之；其次，畏之；其次，侮之。
信不足焉，有不信焉。
悠兮其貴言。
功成事遂，百姓皆謂：我自然。
---
With the best rulers, those below know only that they exist.
Next come rulers the people love and praise; next, rulers they fear; next, rulers they despise.
When trust is not enough, there is distrust.
Unhurried, the best ruler values his words.
When the work is done and things go well, the people all say: “We did it naturally, by ourselves.”
:::

::: parallel 18
大道廢，有仁義；
智慧出，有大偽；
六親不和，有孝慈；
國家昏亂，有忠臣。
---
When the Great Dao is abandoned, there is humaneness and righteousness.
When cleverness and knowledge appear, there is great hypocrisy.
When the six family relations are not in harmony, there is filial piety and parental love.
When the state is in confusion and disorder, there are loyal ministers.
:::

::: parallel 19
絕聖棄智，民利百倍；
絕仁棄義，民復孝慈；
絕巧棄利，盜賊無有。
此三者，以為文不足，故令有所屬：
見素抱樸，少私寡欲。
---
Cut off sageliness, discard cleverness, and the people will benefit a hundredfold.
Cut off humaneness, discard righteousness, and the people will return to filial love.
Cut off ingenuity, discard profit, and there will be no thieves.
These three are mere ornament and not enough, so let there be something to hold to:
show plainness and embrace the uncarved; lessen self-interest and reduce desires.
:::

::: parallel 20
絕學無憂。
唯之與阿，相去幾何？善之與惡，相去若何？
人之所畏，不可不畏。荒兮其未央哉！
眾人熙熙，如享太牢，如春登臺。
我獨泊兮其未兆，如嬰兒之未孩；儽儽兮若無所歸。
眾人皆有餘，而我獨若遺。我愚人之心也哉！沌沌兮。
俗人昭昭，我獨昏昏。俗人察察，我獨悶悶。
澹兮其若海，飂兮若無止。
眾人皆有以，而我獨頑似鄙。
我獨異於人，而貴食母。
---
Cut off learning and there will be no worry.
“Yes” and “yeah”: how far apart are they? Good and bad: how far apart are they?
What others fear, I cannot fail to fear. How vast, and still without end!
The crowd is merry, as if feasting on a great sacrifice, as if climbing a terrace in spring.
I alone am placid, giving no sign, like an infant that has not yet smiled; listless, as if I had nowhere to go.
Everyone has more than enough; I alone seem to have lost out. Mine is the mind of a fool, so muddled!
Ordinary people are bright; I alone am dim. Ordinary people are sharp; I alone am dull.
Calm, like the sea; drifting, as if never stopping.
Everyone has a purpose; I alone am stubborn and uncouth.
I alone am different from others: I value being fed by the mother.
:::

::: parallel 21
孔德之容，惟道是從。
道之為物，惟恍惟惚。
惚兮恍兮，其中有象；恍兮惚兮，其中有物。
窈兮冥兮，其中有精；其精甚真，其中有信。
自古及今，其名不去，以閱眾甫。
吾何以知眾甫之狀哉？以此。
---
The bearing of great virtue follows the Dao alone.
The Dao, as a thing, is elusive and evanescent.
Evanescent, elusive, yet within it there is an image. Elusive, evanescent, yet within it there is a thing.
Dim and dark, yet within it there is an essence. That essence is utterly real; within it there is trustworthiness.
From ancient times to now its name has not departed, and by it we survey the origin of all.
How do I know what the origin of all is like? By this.
:::

::: parallel 22
曲則全，枉則直，窪則盈，敝則新，少則得，多則惑。
是以聖人抱一為天下式。
不自見，故明；不自是，故彰；
不自伐，故有功；不自矜，故長。
夫唯不爭，故天下莫能與之爭。
古之所謂曲則全者，豈虛言哉！誠全而歸之。
---
Bent, then whole; crooked, then straight; hollow, then full; worn out, then new; having little, then gaining; having much, then confused.
So the sage embraces the One and becomes a model for the world.
He does not display himself, so he shines; he does not assert himself, so he stands out;
he does not boast, so he has merit; he does not brag, so he endures.
Only because he does not contend, no one in the world can contend with him.
The old saying “bent, then whole”: are these empty words? Truly, wholeness returns to him.
:::

::: parallel 23
希言自然。
故飄風不終朝，驟雨不終日。
孰為此者？天地。天地尚不能久，而況於人乎？
故從事於道者，道者同於道，德者同於德，失者同於失。
同於道者，道亦樂得之；同於德者，德亦樂得之；同於失者，失亦樂得之。
信不足焉，有不信焉。
---
To speak little is natural.
A whirlwind does not last the whole morning; a downpour does not last the whole day.
Who makes them? Heaven and Earth. If even Heaven and Earth cannot keep it up for long, how much less can people?
So whoever follows the Dao becomes one with the Dao; with virtue, one with virtue; with loss, one with loss.
One with the Dao, the Dao gladly receives him; one with virtue, virtue gladly receives him; one with loss, loss gladly receives him.
When trust is not enough, there is distrust.
:::

::: parallel 24
企者不立；跨者不行；
自見者不明；自是者不彰；
自伐者無功；自矜者不長。
其在道也，曰餘食贅形。
物或惡之，故有道者不處。
---
Stand on tiptoe and you cannot stand firm; take huge strides and you cannot walk far.
Who displays himself does not shine; who asserts himself does not stand out;
who boasts has no merit; who brags does not endure.
From the point of view of the Dao, these are leftover food and growths on the body.
Things are likely to loathe them, so one who has the Dao does not dwell in them.
:::

::: parallel 25
有物混成，先天地生。
寂兮寥兮，獨立而不改，周行而不殆，可以為天下母。
吾不知其名，字之曰道，強為之名曰大。
大曰逝，逝曰遠，遠曰反。
故道大，天大，地大，王亦大。
域中有四大，而王居其一焉。
人法地，地法天，天法道，道法自然。
---
There is a thing formed in chaos, born before Heaven and Earth.
Silent! Empty! Standing alone and unchanging, moving everywhere and never tiring, it could be the mother of the world.
I do not know its name. I style it “Dao”; forced to name it, I call it “great”.
Great means passing on; passing on means going far; going far means returning.
So the Dao is great, Heaven is great, Earth is great, and the king too is great.
In the realm there are four greats, and the king is one of them.
Humans follow Earth, Earth follows Heaven, Heaven follows the Dao, and the Dao follows what is so of itself.
:::

::: parallel 26
重為輕根，靜為躁君。
是以君子終日行不離輜重。
雖有榮觀，燕處超然。
奈何萬乘之主，而以身輕天下？
輕則失根，躁則失君。
---
The heavy is the root of the light; stillness is the master of agitation.
So the noble person travels all day without leaving the baggage carts.
Though there are splendid sights, he stays at ease, above them.
How can the lord of ten thousand chariots treat himself more lightly than the world?
Be light and you lose the root; be agitated and you lose the master.
:::

::: parallel 27
善行無轍跡，善言無瑕讁；善數不用籌策；
善閉無關楗而不可開，善結無繩約而不可解。
是以聖人常善救人，故無棄人；常善救物，故無棄物。是謂襲明。
故善人者，不善人之師；不善人者，善人之資。
不貴其師，不愛其資，雖智大迷，是謂要妙。
---
Good travelling leaves no tracks; good speech has no flaws; good counting needs no tallies;
a good lock has no bolt yet cannot be opened; a good knot has no cord yet cannot be untied.
So the sage is always good at saving people, and no one is abandoned; always good at saving things, and nothing is abandoned. This is called following the light.
So the good person is the teacher of the not-good, and the not-good person is the material of the good.
Not to value the teacher, not to love the material: however clever, this is great confusion. This is called the essential mystery.
:::

::: parallel 28
知其雄，守其雌，為天下谿。
為天下谿，常德不離，復歸於嬰兒。
知其白，守其黑，為天下式。
為天下式，常德不忒，復歸於無極。
知其榮，守其辱，為天下谷。
為天下谷，常德乃足，復歸於樸。
樸散則為器，聖人用之，則為官長，故大制不割。
---
Know the male, keep to the female, and be the ravine of the world.
Being the ravine of the world, constant virtue will not leave you, and you return to being an infant.
Know the white, keep to the black, and be the model for the world.
Being the model for the world, constant virtue will not err, and you return to the limitless.
Know glory, keep to disgrace, and be the valley of the world.
Being the valley of the world, constant virtue will be sufficient, and you return to the uncarved block.
When the uncarved block is cut up it becomes vessels; the sage uses them and becomes chief of officials. So the great cutting does not cut.
:::

::: parallel 29
將欲取天下而為之，吾見其不得已。
天下神器，不可為也，為者敗之，執者失之。
故物或行或隨；或歔或吹；
或強或羸；或挫或隳。
是以聖人去甚，去奢，去泰。
---
Whoever wants to take the world and act on it, I see that he will not succeed.
The world is a sacred vessel; it cannot be acted on. Whoever acts on it ruins it; whoever grasps it loses it.
So among things, some lead and some follow; some breathe gently and some blow hard;
some are strong and some weak; some break and some fall.
So the sage avoids excess, avoids extravagance, avoids extremes.
:::

::: parallel 30
以道佐人主者，不以兵強天下。其事好還。
師之所處，荊棘生焉。大軍之後，必有凶年。
善有果而已，不敢以取強。
果而勿矜，果而勿伐，果而勿驕。
果而不得已，果而勿強。
物壯則老，是謂不道，不道早已。
---
One who assists a ruler with the Dao does not force the world with arms. Such things tend to come back.
Where armies camp, thorns and brambles grow. After a great war there are sure to be years of famine.
The good commander achieves his result and stops; he does not dare to seize by force.
Achieve the result but do not brag, achieve it but do not boast, achieve it but do not be proud.
Achieve it only because there is no choice; achieve it but do not force.
Things that reach their prime grow old. This is called contrary to the Dao, and what is contrary to the Dao soon ends.
:::

::: parallel 31
夫佳兵者，不祥之器，物或惡之，故有道者不處。
君子居則貴左，用兵則貴右。
兵者不祥之器，非君子之器，不得已而用之，恬淡為上。
勝而不美，而美之者，是樂殺人。
夫樂殺人者，則不可以得志於天下矣。
吉事尚左，凶事尚右。偏將軍居左，上將軍居右。言以喪禮處之。
殺人之眾，以哀悲泣之，戰勝以喪禮處之。
---
Fine weapons are instruments of ill omen. Things are likely to loathe them, so one who has the Dao does not dwell with them.
At home the noble person honours the left; at war he honours the right.
Weapons are instruments of ill omen, not the instruments of the noble person. He uses them only when he has no choice, and calm restraint is best.
Victory is not beautiful. Whoever finds it beautiful delights in killing people,
and whoever delights in killing people cannot have his way in the world.
On happy occasions the left is honoured; on sad occasions, the right. The lieutenant general stands on the left, the commanding general on the right. This means war is treated as a funeral.
When many people have been killed, weep for them with grief and sorrow. A victory in war is treated with the rites of mourning.
:::

::: parallel 32
道常無名。樸雖小，天下莫能臣也。
侯王若能守之，萬物將自賓。
天地相合，以降甘露，民莫之令而自均。
始制有名，名亦既有，夫亦將知止，知止可以不殆。
譬道之在天下，猶川谷之於江海。
---
The Dao is constant and nameless. Though the uncarved block is small, no one in the world can make it a subject.
If lords and kings could hold to it, the ten thousand things would come to them as guests of their own accord.
Heaven and Earth would join to send down sweet dew, and the people, without being ordered, would share it fairly.
When cutting begins there are names. Once there are names, one must know when to stop. Knowing when to stop, one is free from danger.
The Dao's place in the world is like that of rivers and streams flowing to the sea.
:::

::: parallel 33
知人者智，自知者明。
勝人者有力，自勝者強。
知足者富。強行者有志。
不失其所者久。死而不亡者壽。
---
Who knows others is clever; who knows himself is clear-sighted.
Who conquers others has force; who conquers himself is strong.
Who knows he has enough is rich. Who perseveres has will.
Who does not lose his place endures. Who dies but does not perish lives long.
:::

::: parallel 34
大道氾兮，其可左右。
萬物恃之而生而不辭，功成不名有。
衣養萬物而不為主，常無欲，可名於小；
萬物歸焉，而不為主，可名為大。
以其終不自為大，故能成其大。
---
The Great Dao flows everywhere; it can go left or right.
The ten thousand things rely on it for life and it does not turn them away; it completes its work and claims nothing.
It clothes and feeds the ten thousand things but does not act as their master. Always without desire, it can be called small.
The ten thousand things return to it, yet it does not act as their master: it can be called great.
Because it never makes itself great, it can achieve its greatness.
:::

::: parallel 35
執大象，天下往。
往而不害，安平太。
樂與餌，過客止。
道之出口，淡乎其無味，
視之不足見，聽之不足聞，用之不足既。
---
Hold to the great image, and the whole world will come.
They come and are not harmed; there is safety, peace and calm.
Music and food make passing travellers stop.
But the Dao, when spoken of, is bland and without flavour.
Looked at, it is not enough to see; listened to, not enough to hear; used, it cannot be used up.
:::

::: parallel 36
將欲歙之，必固張之；將欲弱之，必固強之；
將欲廢之，必固興之；將欲奪之，必固與之。
是謂微明。柔弱勝剛強。
魚不可脫於淵，國之利器不可以示人。
---
What is to be shrunk must first be stretched; what is to be weakened must first be strengthened;
what is to be cast down must first be raised up; what is to be taken must first be given.
This is called subtle clarity. The soft and weak overcome the hard and strong.
Fish should not leave the deep water; the sharp instruments of a state should not be shown to people.
:::

::: parallel 37
道常無為而無不為。
侯王若能守之，萬物將自化。
化而欲作，吾將鎮之以無名之樸。
無名之樸，夫亦將無欲。
不欲以靜，天下將自定。
---
The Dao constantly does nothing, yet nothing is left undone.
If lords and kings could hold to it, the ten thousand things would transform by themselves.
If, transforming, desire should stir, I would calm it with the nameless uncarved block.
The nameless uncarved block brings freedom from desire.
Without desire there is stillness, and the world will settle by itself.
:::

## Book Two · Virtue (chapters 38–81)

::: parallel 38
上德不德，是以有德；下德不失德，是以無德。
上德無為而無以為；下德為之而有以為。
上仁為之而無以為；上義為之而有以為。
上禮為之而莫之應，則攘臂而扔之。
故失道而後德，失德而後仁，失仁而後義，失義而後禮。
夫禮者，忠信之薄，而亂之首。
前識者，道之華，而愚之始。
是以大丈夫處其厚，不居其薄；處其實，不居其華。故去彼取此。
---
Highest virtue is not virtuous, and so it has virtue. Lowest virtue never lets go of virtue, and so it has none.
Highest virtue does nothing and has no motive for doing; lowest virtue acts and has a motive.
Highest humaneness acts but has no motive; highest righteousness acts and has a motive.
Highest ritual acts, and when no one responds, it rolls up its sleeves and drags them along.
So when the Dao is lost there is virtue; when virtue is lost, humaneness; when humaneness is lost, righteousness; when righteousness is lost, ritual.
Ritual is the thinning of loyalty and trust, and the beginning of disorder.
Foreknowledge is the flower of the Dao and the start of folly.
So the great person dwells in the thick, not the thin; in the fruit, not the flower. He lets go of that and takes this.
:::

::: parallel 39
昔之得一者：
天得一以清；地得一以寧；神得一以靈；
谷得一以盈；萬物得一以生；侯王得一以為天下貞。
其致之。
天無以清，將恐裂；地無以寧，將恐發；神無以靈，將恐歇；
谷無以盈，將恐竭；萬物無以生，將恐滅；侯王無以貴高，將恐蹶。
故貴以賤為本，高以下為基。
是以侯王自稱孤、寡、不穀。此非以賤為本邪？非乎？
故致數輿無輿。不欲琭琭如玉，珞珞如石。
---
Of old, these attained the One:
Heaven attained the One and became clear; Earth attained the One and became calm; the spirits attained the One and became numinous;
the valley attained the One and became full; the ten thousand things attained the One and came to life; lords and kings attained the One and became the standard of the world.
All this came from the One.
If Heaven were not clear, it might split; if Earth were not calm, it might erupt; if the spirits were not numinous, they might fade away;
if the valley were not full, it might run dry; if the ten thousand things had no life, they might perish; if lords and kings were not noble and high, they might fall.
So the noble takes the humble as its root; the high takes the low as its foundation.
That is why lords and kings call themselves “the orphan”, “the lonely one”, “the unworthy”. Is this not taking the humble as the root? Is it not?
So count the parts of a carriage and there is no carriage. Do not wish to tinkle like jade; rumble like stone.
:::

::: parallel 40
反者道之動；弱者道之用。
天下萬物生於有，有生於無。
---
Returning is the movement of the Dao; weakness is the function of the Dao.
The ten thousand things of the world are born from being, and being is born from non-being.
:::

::: parallel 41
上士聞道，勤而行之；中士聞道，若存若亡；
下士聞道，大笑之。不笑不足以為道。
故建言有之：
明道若昧；進道若退；夷道若纇；
上德若谷；大白若辱；廣德若不足；
建德若偷；質真若渝；大方無隅；
大器晚成；大音希聲；大象無形；
道隱無名。夫唯道，善貸且成。
---
When the highest adept hears of the Dao, he practises it diligently. When the middling adept hears of the Dao, he half believes it.
When the lowest adept hears of the Dao, he laughs out loud. If he did not laugh, it would not be the Dao.
So the old sayings have it:
The bright Dao seems dark; the Dao that advances seems to retreat; the level Dao seems rough.
Highest virtue seems a valley; the purest white seems soiled; ample virtue seems lacking;
firm virtue seems furtive; true substance seems changeable; the great square has no corners;
the great vessel is completed late; the great note is faint of sound; the great image has no shape.
The Dao is hidden and nameless. Yet only the Dao is good at lending and at bringing to completion.
:::

::: parallel 42
道生一，一生二，二生三，三生萬物。
萬物負陰而抱陽，沖氣以為和。
人之所惡，唯孤、寡、不穀，而王公以為稱。
故物或損之而益，或益之而損。
人之所教，我亦教之。
強梁者不得其死，吾將以為教父。
---
The Dao gives birth to one, one gives birth to two, two gives birth to three, and three gives birth to the ten thousand things.
The ten thousand things carry yin on their backs and embrace yang, and blending breath makes them harmonious.
What people hate most is to be orphaned, lonely and unworthy, yet kings and lords call themselves by these names.
So things may gain by losing, or lose by gaining.
What others teach, I teach as well:
“The violent do not die a natural death.” I will make this the father of my teaching.
:::

::: parallel 43
天下之至柔，馳騁天下之至堅。
無有入無間，吾是以知無為之有益。
不言之教，無為之益，天下希及之。
---
The softest thing in the world gallops over the hardest.
What has no substance enters where there is no gap. By this I know the benefit of non-action.
The teaching without words and the benefit of non-action: few in the world attain them.
:::

::: parallel 44
名與身孰親？身與貨孰多？得與亡孰病？
是故甚愛必大費；多藏必厚亡。
知足不辱，知止不殆，可以長久。
---
Fame or your self: which is dearer? Your self or your goods: which is worth more? Gain or loss: which is worse?
So great attachment must cost dearly; great hoarding must bring heavy loss.
Know what is enough and you will not be disgraced. Know when to stop and you will not be in danger. You can last long.
:::

::: parallel 45
大成若缺，其用不弊。
大盈若沖，其用不窮。
大直若屈，大巧若拙，大辯若訥。
躁勝寒，靜勝熱。清靜為天下正。
---
Great completion seems lacking, yet its use is never exhausted.
Great fullness seems empty, yet its use is never used up.
Great straightness seems bent; great skill seems clumsy; great eloquence seems tongue-tied.
Movement overcomes cold; stillness overcomes heat. Clarity and stillness set the world right.
:::

::: parallel 46
天下有道，卻走馬以糞。
天下無道，戎馬生於郊。
禍莫大於不知足；咎莫大於欲得。
故知足之足，常足矣。
---
When the world has the Dao, swift horses are turned back to pull dung carts.
When the world lacks the Dao, war horses are bred outside the cities.
No disaster is greater than not knowing what is enough; no fault is greater than wanting to get.
So the sufficiency of knowing what is enough is lasting sufficiency.
:::

::: parallel 47
不出戶，知天下；不闚牖，見天道。
其出彌遠，其知彌少。
是以聖人不行而知，不見而名，不為而成。
---
Without going out of the door, know the world. Without looking out of the window, see the Way of Heaven.
The farther one goes, the less one knows.
So the sage knows without travelling, names without seeing, and accomplishes without acting.
:::

::: parallel 48
為學日益，為道日損。
損之又損，以至於無為。
無為而無不為。
取天下常以無事，及其有事，不足以取天下。
---
Pursuing learning, one gains every day. Pursuing the Dao, one loses every day.
Losing and losing again, one arrives at non-action.
Doing nothing, nothing is left undone.
The world is always won by not meddling. Once one meddles, one is not fit to win the world.
:::

::: parallel 49
聖人無常心，以百姓心為心。
善者，吾善之；不善者，吾亦善之；德善。
信者，吾信之；不信者，吾亦信之；德信。
聖人在天下，歙歙焉，為天下渾其心，
百姓皆注其耳目，聖人皆孩之。
---
The sage has no fixed mind; he takes the mind of the people as his mind.
The good I treat as good; the not-good I also treat as good: so goodness is gained.
The trustworthy I trust; the untrustworthy I also trust: so trust is gained.
The sage in the world draws in, and for the world's sake keeps his mind undivided.
The people all strain their eyes and ears; the sage treats them all as his children.
:::

::: parallel 50
出生入死。
生之徒，十有三；死之徒，十有三；
人之生，動之於死地，亦十有三。
夫何故？以其生生之厚。
蓋聞善攝生者，陸行不遇兕虎，入軍不被甲兵；
兕無所投其角，虎無所用其爪，兵無所容其刃。
夫何故？以其無死地。
---
We come out into life and go into death.
The companions of life are thirteen; the companions of death are thirteen;
and people who, in living, move towards the place of death are also thirteen.
Why is this? Because they hold on to life too tightly.
I have heard that one who is good at preserving life travels by land without meeting rhinoceros or tiger, and enters battle without armour or weapons.
The rhinoceros finds nowhere to thrust its horn, the tiger nowhere to sink its claws, the weapon nowhere to lodge its blade.
Why is this? Because he has no place of death.
:::

::: parallel 51
道生之，德畜之，物形之，勢成之。
是以萬物莫不尊道而貴德。
道之尊，德之貴，夫莫之命而常自然。
故道生之，德畜之；長之育之；亭之毒之；養之覆之。
生而不有，為而不恃，長而不宰。是謂玄德。
---
The Dao gives them birth, virtue rears them, things give them shape, and circumstance completes them.
So all the ten thousand things honour the Dao and value virtue.
The Dao is honoured and virtue valued not because anyone commands it but because it is always so of itself.
So the Dao gives birth and virtue rears; they grow them and raise them, shelter and nurture them, feed and protect them.
To give birth without possessing, to act without relying on it, to lead without ruling: this is called mysterious virtue.
:::

::: parallel 52
天下有始，以為天下母。
既得其母，以知其子，既知其子，復守其母，沒身不殆。
塞其兌，閉其門，終身不勤。
開其兌，濟其事，終身不救。
見小曰明，守柔曰強。
用其光，復歸其明，無遺身殃；是為習常。
---
The world has a beginning, which is the mother of the world.
Having found the mother, know the children; knowing the children, hold fast to the mother again, and to the end of life you will be in no danger.
Block the openings, shut the doors, and all your life you will not be worn out.
Open the openings, busy yourself with affairs, and all your life you will be beyond help.
Seeing the small is called clarity; keeping to the soft is called strength.
Use the light, return to clarity, and bring no harm upon yourself: this is called practising the constant.
:::

::: parallel 53
使我介然有知，行於大道，唯施是畏。
大道甚夷，而人好徑。
朝甚除，田甚蕪，倉甚虛；
服文綵，帶利劍，厭飲食，財貨有餘；
是為盜夸。非道也哉！
---
If I had even a little knowledge, walking the Great Way, my only fear would be of straying.
The Great Way is very smooth, yet people love the side paths.
The court is spotless, the fields are full of weeds, the granaries are empty;
yet they wear embroidered clothes, carry sharp swords, are sated with food and drink, and have more wealth than they can use.
This is called the boasting of robbers. It is surely not the Dao!
:::

::: parallel 54
善建者不拔，善抱者不脫，子孫以祭祀不輟。
修之於身，其德乃真；修之於家，其德乃餘；
修之於鄉，其德乃長；修之於國，其德乃豐；
修之於天下，其德乃普。
故以身觀身，以家觀家，以鄉觀鄉，以國觀國，以天下觀天下。
吾何以知天下然哉？以此。
---
What is well planted is not uprooted; what is well embraced does not slip away; and the sacrifices of children and grandchildren will never cease.
Cultivate it in yourself and its virtue is genuine; cultivate it in the family and its virtue is abundant;
cultivate it in the village and its virtue lasts; cultivate it in the state and its virtue flourishes;
cultivate it in the world and its virtue is universal.
So view the self through the self, the family through the family, the village through the village, the state through the state, and the world through the world.
How do I know the world is so? By this.
:::

::: parallel 55
含德之厚，比於赤子。
蜂蠆虺蛇不螫，猛獸不據，攫鳥不搏。
骨弱筋柔而握固。
未知牝牡之合而全作，精之至也。
終日號而不嗄，和之至也。
知和曰常，知常曰明，益生曰祥，心使氣曰強。
物壯則老，謂之不道，不道早已。
---
One who holds virtue in abundance is like a newborn child.
Wasps, scorpions and snakes do not sting it; wild beasts do not seize it; birds of prey do not strike it.
Its bones are weak and its sinews soft, yet its grip is firm.
It knows nothing yet of the union of male and female, yet it becomes aroused: its vital essence is at its height.
It cries all day without growing hoarse: its harmony is at its height.
Knowing harmony is called the constant; knowing the constant is called clarity. Forcing life is called ill-omened; letting the mind drive the breath is called forcing.
Things that reach their prime grow old. This is called contrary to the Dao, and what is contrary to the Dao soon ends.
:::

::: parallel 56
知者不言，言者不知。
塞其兌，閉其門，挫其銳，解其紛，和其光，同其塵，是謂玄同。
故不可得而親，不可得而疏；
不可得而利，不可得而害；
不可得而貴，不可得而賤。故為天下貴。
---
Those who know do not speak; those who speak do not know.
Block the openings, shut the doors, blunt the sharp, untangle the knots, soften the glare, become one with the dust: this is called mysterious oneness.
Such a person cannot be drawn close, and cannot be kept at a distance;
cannot be benefited, and cannot be harmed;
cannot be ennobled, and cannot be debased. So he is the most valued in the world.
:::

::: parallel 57
以正治國，以奇用兵，以無事取天下。
吾何以知其然哉？以此：
天下多忌諱，而民彌貧；民多利器，國家滋昏；
人多伎巧，奇物滋起；法令滋彰，盜賊多有。
故聖人云：我無為，而民自化；我好靜，而民自正；
我無事，而民自富；我無欲，而民自樸。
---
Govern the state by the straightforward, wage war by surprise, and win the world by not meddling.
How do I know it is so? By this:
The more prohibitions in the world, the poorer the people; the more sharp tools the people have, the more confused the state;
the more cunning skills people have, the more strange things appear; the more laws and orders are posted, the more thieves and robbers there are.
So the sage says: I do nothing, and the people transform themselves; I love stillness, and the people set themselves right;
I do not meddle, and the people grow rich by themselves; I have no desires, and the people become simple by themselves.
:::

::: parallel 58
其政悶悶，其民淳淳；其政察察，其民缺缺。
禍兮福之所倚，福兮禍之所伏。
孰知其極？其無正。
正復為奇，善復為妖。人之迷，其日固久。
是以聖人方而不割，廉而不劌，直而不肆，光而不燿。
---
When government is muddled, the people are simple; when government is sharp-eyed, the people are crafty.
Misfortune is what good fortune leans on; good fortune is where misfortune hides.
Who knows where it ends? There is no fixed standard.
The straightforward turns into the strange; the good turns into the uncanny. People have been confused about this for a long time.
So the sage is square but does not cut, sharp-edged but does not wound, straight but not overbearing, bright but not dazzling.
:::

::: parallel 59
治人事天，莫若嗇。
夫唯嗇，是謂早服；
早服謂之重積德；重積德則無不克；
無不克則莫知其極；莫知其極，可以有國；
有國之母，可以長久；
是謂深根固柢，長生久視之道。
---
In governing people and serving Heaven, nothing is better than frugality.
Only frugality allows one to submit early;
submitting early is called accumulating virtue twice over; with virtue twice accumulated there is nothing one cannot overcome;
when there is nothing one cannot overcome, no one knows one's limit; when no one knows one's limit, one can possess the state;
possessing the mother of the state, one can last long.
This is called deep roots and a firm stem, the Way of long life and lasting vision.
:::

::: parallel 60
治大國，若烹小鮮。
以道蒞天下，其鬼不神；
非其鬼不神，其神不傷人；
非其神不傷人，聖人亦不傷人。
夫兩不相傷，故德交歸焉。
---
Govern a great state as you would cook a small fish.
When the world is ruled with the Dao, ghosts have no power.
It is not that ghosts have no power, but that their power does not harm people.
It is not only that their power does not harm people; the sage also does not harm people.
Since neither harms the other, virtue flows back to both.
:::

::: parallel 61
大國者下流，天下之交，天下之牝。
牝常以靜勝牡，以靜為下。
故大國以下小國，則取小國；小國以下大國，則取大國。
故或下以取，或下而取。
大國不過欲兼畜人，小國不過欲入事人。
夫兩者各得所欲，大者宜為下。
---
A great state is like the lower reaches of a river, where the world meets: the female of the world.
The female always overcomes the male by stillness, and in stillness takes the lower place.
So a great state that places itself below a small state wins the small state; a small state that places itself below a great state wins the great state.
So one takes the low place in order to win, the other is low and so wins.
The great state only wants to unite and care for people; the small state only wants to join and serve.
For both to get what they want, the great one should take the lower place.
:::

::: parallel 62
道者萬物之奧。
善人之寶，不善人之所保。
美言可以市尊，美行可以加人。
人之不善，何棄之有？
故立天子，置三公，雖有拱璧以先駟馬，不如坐進此道。
古之所以貴此道者何？
不曰：以求得，有罪以免邪？故為天下貴。
---
The Dao is the hidden heart of the ten thousand things,
the treasure of the good person and the refuge of the person who is not good.
Fine words can buy honour; fine deeds can raise a person up.
Even if a person is not good, why abandon them?
So when the Son of Heaven is enthroned and the three ministers installed, though there be jade discs held in both hands followed by teams of four horses, it is better to sit still and offer this Dao.
Why did the ancients value this Dao?
Is it not said: “Seek and you will find; have faults and you will be spared”? So it is the most valued in the world.
:::

::: parallel 63
為無為，事無事，味無味。
大小多少，報怨以德。
圖難於其易，為大於其細；
天下難事，必作於易；天下大事，必作於細。
是以聖人終不為大，故能成其大。
夫輕諾必寡信，多易必多難。
是以聖人猶難之，故終無難矣。
---
Act by non-action; work by not working; taste the tasteless.
Treat the small as great and the few as many. Repay resentment with virtue.
Plan for the difficult while it is easy; do the great while it is small.
The difficult things of the world must begin with the easy; the great things of the world must begin with the small.
So the sage never does anything great, and so he can achieve greatness.
Light promises inspire little trust; thinking things easy brings many difficulties.
So the sage treats things as difficult, and in the end has no difficulty.
:::

::: parallel 64
其安易持，其未兆易謀。
其脆易泮，其微易散。
為之於未有，治之於未亂。
合抱之木，生於毫末；九層之臺，起於累土；千里之行，始於足下。
為者敗之，執者失之。
是以聖人無為故無敗，無執故無失。
民之從事，常於幾成而敗之。
慎終如始，則無敗事。
是以聖人欲不欲，不貴難得之貨；
學不學，復眾人之所過，以輔萬物之自然而不敢為。
---
What is at rest is easy to hold; what has not yet shown itself is easy to plan for.
What is brittle is easy to break; what is minute is easy to scatter.
Act on things before they exist; put them in order before there is disorder.
A tree you can barely put your arms around grows from a tiny shoot; a terrace nine storeys high rises from a heap of earth; a journey of a thousand miles begins beneath your feet.
Whoever acts on it ruins it; whoever grasps it loses it.
So the sage does nothing and so ruins nothing; grasps nothing and so loses nothing.
People at their work often ruin it when it is nearly done.
Be as careful at the end as at the beginning, and nothing will be ruined.
So the sage desires not to desire and does not value goods hard to get;
he learns not to learn and goes back to what the crowd has passed by, to help the ten thousand things be as they are of themselves, without daring to act.
:::

::: parallel 65
古之善為道者，非以明民，將以愚之。
民之難治，以其智多。
故以智治國，國之賊；不以智治國，國之福。
知此兩者亦稽式。常知稽式，是謂玄德。
玄德深矣，遠矣，與物反矣，然後乃至大順。
---
Those of old who were good at the Dao did not use it to make the people clever but to keep them simple.
People are hard to govern because they are too clever.
So to govern a state by cleverness is to rob it; not to govern it by cleverness is to bless it.
To know these two is to know the pattern. Always knowing the pattern is called mysterious virtue.
Mysterious virtue is deep and far-reaching. It goes back with things, and then arrives at the great harmony.
:::

::: parallel 66
江海所以能為百谷王者，以其善下之，故能為百谷王。
是以聖人欲上民，必以言下之；欲先民，必以身後之。
是以聖人處上而民不重，處前而民不害。
是以天下樂推而不厭。
以其不爭，故天下莫能與之爭。
---
Rivers and seas can be king of the hundred valleys because they are good at staying below them. That is why they can be king of the hundred valleys.
So if the sage wants to be above the people, he must speak as if below them; if he wants to lead them, he must put himself behind.
So the sage is above and the people do not feel his weight; he is in front and the people do not feel harmed.
So the whole world gladly pushes him forward and never tires of him.
Because he does not contend, no one in the world can contend with him.
:::

::: parallel 67
天下皆謂我道大，似不肖。
夫唯大，故似不肖。若肖，久矣其細也夫！
我有三寶，持而保之。
一曰慈，二曰儉，三曰不敢為天下先。
慈故能勇；儉故能廣；不敢為天下先，故能成器長。
今舍慈且勇；舍儉且廣；舍後且先；死矣！
夫慈以戰則勝，以守則固。
天將救之，以慈衛之。
---
Everyone in the world says my Dao is great, yet it seems unlike anything.
It is because it is great that it seems unlike anything. If it were like something, it would long ago have become small.
I have three treasures that I hold and keep.
The first is compassion, the second is frugality, the third is not daring to be first in the world.
Compassionate, one can be brave; frugal, one can be generous; not daring to be first in the world, one can become leader of all.
Now to give up compassion yet be brave, to give up frugality yet be generous, to give up being last yet be first: this is death!
With compassion one wins in attack and holds firm in defence.
When Heaven means to save someone, it protects them with compassion.
:::

::: parallel 68
善為士者，不武；善戰者，不怒；
善勝敵者，不與；善用人者，為之下。
是謂不爭之德，是謂用人之力，
是謂配天古之極。
---
A good officer is not warlike; a good fighter is not angry;
one who is good at defeating the enemy does not engage him; one who is good at using people puts himself below them.
This is called the virtue of not contending; this is called using the strength of others;
this is called matching Heaven, the ancient ideal.
:::

::: parallel 69
用兵有言：吾不敢為主，而為客；不敢進寸，而退尺。
是謂行無行；攘無臂；扔無敵；執無兵。
禍莫大於輕敵，輕敵幾喪吾寶。
故抗兵相加，哀者勝矣。
---
Strategists have a saying: “I dare not be the host but would rather be the guest; I dare not advance an inch but would rather retreat a foot.”
This is called marching without marching, rolling up sleeves without arms, dragging along without an enemy, holding weapons without weapons.
No disaster is greater than underestimating the enemy; underestimating the enemy, I nearly lose my treasures.
So when opposing armies meet, the side that grieves will win.
:::

::: parallel 70
吾言甚易知，甚易行。
天下莫能知，莫能行。
言有宗，事有君。
夫唯無知，是以不我知。
知我者希，則我者貴。
是以聖人被褐懷玉。
---
My words are very easy to understand and very easy to practise.
Yet no one in the world can understand them, and no one can practise them.
Words have an ancestor; deeds have a master.
Only because people do not understand this do they not understand me.
Those who understand me are few; those who follow me are rare treasures.
So the sage wears coarse cloth and carries jade inside.
:::

::: parallel 71
知不知，上；不知知，病。
夫唯病病，是以不病。
聖人不病，以其病病，是以不病。
---
To know that you do not know is highest; not to know, yet think you know, is a sickness.
Only by recognising the sickness as sickness can one be free of it.
The sage is not sick, because he recognises the sickness as sickness. That is why he is not sick.
:::

::: parallel 72
民不畏威，則大威至。
無狎其所居，無厭其所生。
夫唯不厭，是以不厭。
是以聖人自知不自見；自愛不自貴。故去彼取此。
---
When the people no longer fear authority, a greater terror will come.
Do not crowd them in their dwellings; do not oppress them in their livelihoods.
Only if you do not oppress them will they not tire of you.
So the sage knows himself but does not display himself, loves himself but does not exalt himself. He lets go of that and takes this.
:::

::: parallel 73
勇於敢則殺，勇於不敢則活。
此兩者，或利或害。
天之所惡，孰知其故？是以聖人猶難之。
天之道，不爭而善勝，不言而善應，不召而自來，繟然而善謀。
天網恢恢，疏而不失。
---
Brave in daring, one is killed; brave in not daring, one lives.
Of these two, one benefits and one harms.
Heaven hates what it hates: who knows why? Even the sage finds this difficult.
The Way of Heaven is to win without contending, to respond without speaking, to arrive without being summoned, to plan well while seeming unhurried.
Heaven's net is vast; its mesh is wide, yet nothing slips through.
:::

::: parallel 74
民不畏死，奈何以死懼之？
若使民常畏死，而為奇者，吾得執而殺之，孰敢？
常有司殺者殺。
夫代司殺者殺，是謂代大匠斲。
夫代大匠斲者，希有不傷其手矣。
---
If the people do not fear death, why threaten them with death?
If the people always feared death, and I could seize and kill those who do wrong, who would dare?
There is always the official executioner who kills.
To kill in place of the executioner is like cutting wood in place of the master carpenter.
Of those who cut wood in place of the master carpenter, few do not cut their hands.
:::

::: parallel 75
民之饑，以其上食稅之多，是以饑。
民之難治，以其上之有為，是以難治。
民之輕死，以其求生之厚，是以輕死。
夫唯無以生為者，是賢於貴生。
---
The people starve because those above eat up too much in taxes. That is why they starve.
The people are hard to govern because those above are always doing things. That is why they are hard to govern.
The people take death lightly because those above seek life so greedily. That is why they take death lightly.
Only one who does not make life his goal is wiser than one who prizes life.
:::

::: parallel 76
人之生也柔弱，其死也堅強。
萬物草木之生也柔脆，其死也枯槁。
故堅強者死之徒，柔弱者生之徒。
是以兵強則不勝，木強則兵。
強大處下，柔弱處上。
---
When people are alive they are soft and supple; when they die they are hard and stiff.
When plants and trees are alive they are tender and pliant; when they die they are withered and dry.
So the hard and stiff are companions of death; the soft and supple are companions of life.
So an army that is too strong will not win; a tree that is too strong will be felled.
The strong and great take the lower place; the soft and supple take the higher.
:::

::: parallel 77
天之道，其猶張弓與？
高者抑之，下者舉之；有餘者損之，不足者補之。
天之道，損有餘而補不足。
人之道，則不然，損不足以奉有餘。
孰能有餘以奉天下？唯有道者。
是以聖人為而不恃，功成而不處，其不欲見賢。
---
Is not the Way of Heaven like drawing a bow?
What is high is pressed down, what is low is raised; what has too much is reduced, what has too little is supplemented.
The Way of Heaven reduces what has too much and supplements what has too little.
The way of humans is not so: it takes from those who have too little to give to those who have too much.
Who can take what he has in excess and give it to the world? Only one who has the Dao.
So the sage acts without relying on it, completes his work without dwelling in it, and has no wish to show his worth.
:::

::: parallel 78
天下莫柔弱於水，而攻堅強者莫之能勝，以其無以易之。
弱之勝強，柔之勝剛，天下莫不知，莫能行。
是以聖人云：受國之垢，是謂社稷主；
受國不祥，是為天下王。
正言若反。
---
Nothing in the world is softer or weaker than water, yet for attacking the hard and strong nothing surpasses it, because nothing can take its place.
That the weak overcomes the strong and the soft overcomes the hard, everyone in the world knows, yet no one can practise it.
So the sage says: one who takes on the filth of the state is called lord of its altars;
one who takes on the misfortunes of the state is called king of the world.
True words seem contrary.
:::

::: parallel 79
和大怨，必有餘怨；安可以為善？
是以聖人執左契，而不責於人。
有德司契，無德司徹。
天道無親，常與善人。
---
When a great resentment is settled, some resentment is sure to remain. How can this be counted as good?
So the sage holds the left half of the tally but makes no demands on others.
The virtuous attend to the tally; those without virtue attend to collecting.
The Way of Heaven has no favourites; it is always with the good person.
:::

::: parallel 80
小國寡民。
使有什伯之器而不用；使民重死而不遠徙。
雖有舟輿，無所乘之，雖有甲兵，無所陳之。
使民復結繩而用之，
甘其食，美其服，安其居，樂其俗。
鄰國相望，雞犬之聲相聞，民至老死，不相往來。
---
A small state with few people.
Let there be tools for tens and hundreds of people, yet let them go unused; let the people take death seriously and not travel far.
Though they have boats and carriages, let there be no occasion to ride them; though they have armour and weapons, let there be no occasion to display them.
Let the people go back to knotting cords to keep records.
Let them find their food sweet, their clothes beautiful, their homes peaceful and their customs a joy.
Neighbouring states may be within sight of each other, and the sounds of their cocks and dogs may be heard across the way, yet the people grow old and die without ever visiting one another.
:::

::: parallel 81
信言不美，美言不信。
善者不辯，辯者不善。
知者不博，博者不知。
聖人不積，既以為人，己愈有；既以與人，己愈多。
天之道，利而不害；聖人之道，為而不爭。
---
True words are not beautiful; beautiful words are not true.
The good do not argue; those who argue are not good.
Those who know are not learned; the learned do not know.
The sage does not hoard. The more he does for others, the more he has; the more he gives to others, the more he possesses.
The Way of Heaven is to benefit and not to harm. The Way of the sage is to act and not to contend.
:::
