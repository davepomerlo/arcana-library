::: note About this text
The *Nihon shoki* 日本書紀 ("Chronicles of Japan", also called *Nihongi*), presented to the court in 720, eight years after the *Kojiki*, was written in Chinese for an audience that included the courts of China and Korea. It begins, like the *Kojiki*, with the age of the gods (*kamiyo* 神代), but its first sentences are a patchwork of Chinese cosmology.

Two Chinese sources can be identified. The image of chaos "like a hen's egg", with the clear rising and the heavy sinking, follows the *Sanwu liji* 三五曆紀, a lost 3rd-century work by Xu Zheng 徐整 that survives only in quotations, such as those in the Song encyclopedia *Taiping yulan*: «未有天地之時，混沌狀如雞子» ("before there were heaven and earth, chaos was shaped like a hen's egg"). The sentences about the clear uniting easily and the heavy hardening with difficulty, so that heaven was completed first, are copied almost word for word from the *Huainanzi* (see [Heavenly Patterns](huainanzi-heavenly-patterns.html)). Only after that does the text turn to the native Japanese image of a reed shoot sprouting in the drifting land.

The Nihon shoki also records, after its main text, a series of variant traditions (一書 *aru fumi*, "one book says"), so that a reader sees several versions of each myth side by side.
:::

## The separation of heaven and earth

::: parallel 1
古天地未剖、陰陽不分、渾沌如鷄子、溟涬而含牙。
---
In ancient times, when heaven and earth were not yet split apart and yin and yang were not divided, all was chaos, like a hen's egg, dim and vague, yet holding a sprout within.
:::

::: parallel 2
及其清陽者薄靡而爲天、重濁者淹滯而爲地、精妙之合搏易、重濁之凝竭難。故天先成而地後定。
---
Then the clear and bright part trailed upward and became heaven, and the heavy and turbid part settled and became earth. The pure and subtle unite easily; the heavy and turbid harden with difficulty. So heaven was completed first, and earth was settled afterwards.
:::

## The first gods

::: parallel 3
然後、神聖生其中焉。故曰、開闢之初、洲壤浮漂、譬猶游魚之浮水上也。
---
After that, divine beings were born between them. So it is said: at the beginning, when heaven and earth opened, the land drifted and floated, like a fish swimming on the surface of the water.
:::

::: parallel 4
于時、天地之中生一物。狀如葦牙。便化爲神。號國常立尊。次國狹槌尊。次豐斟渟尊。凡三神矣。乾道獨化。所以、成此純男。
---
At that time, between heaven and earth, a thing was born. Its form was like a reed shoot. It turned into a god, called Kuni-no-tokotachi no Mikoto. Next came Kuni-no-satsuchi no Mikoto, and next Toyokumunu no Mikoto: three gods in all. The way of Heaven alone produced them; that is why they were purely male.
:::

::: note Words worth knowing
- **鶏子**: a hen's egg. The cosmic egg appears in creation myths from India to Finland; see the archive's [Kalevala](kalevala-creation.html).
- **葦牙 *ashikabi***: a reed shoot, the image the Kojiki also uses for the god Umashi-ashikabi-hikoji. Reeds sprouting from marshy ground were a natural picture of life appearing in the Japanese landscape.
- **國常立尊 *Kuni-no-tokotachi no Mikoto***: "the god who stands eternally over the land". In the Kojiki he appears only sixth; in medieval Ise Shinto and later Yoshida Shinto he became the supreme, original god.
- **乾道獨化**: "the way of Heaven (*qian*, the pure yang trigram of the Yijing) transforms alone". The first gods are produced by yang alone, without a female partner.
:::
