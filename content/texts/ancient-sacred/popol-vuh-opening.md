::: note About this text
The *Popol Vuh* ("Book of the Council" or "Book of the Community"; in modern K'iche' spelling *Popol Wuj*) is the most important surviving work of Maya literature. It was written in the 1550s by members of the K'iche' nobility, a generation after the Spanish conquest, using the Latin alphabet to record what had been kept in an older book that, they say, "can no longer be seen". The original is lost. The only copy is the one made around 1701 by the Dominican friar Francisco Ximénez in Chichicastenango, with a Spanish translation in a parallel column; it is now in the Newberry Library in Chicago.

The book has three movements: the creation of the world and the failed attempts to make people (of mud, then wood); the adventures of the Hero Twins Hunahpu and Xbalanque in the underworld, Xib'alb'a; and the creation of humans from maize, followed by the history of the K'iche' lineages.
:::

## The authors' preface

::: parallel 1
ARE' U XE' OJER TZIJ, Waral K'iche' u b'i'. WARAL xchiqatz'ib'aj wi, Xchiqatikib'a' wi ojer tzij, U tikarib'al, U xe'nab'al puch, Ronojel xb'an pa Tinamit K'iche', Ramaq' K'iche' winaq.
---
THIS IS THE ROOT OF THE ANCIENT WORD, here in the place called K'iche'. HERE we shall write, we shall plant the ancient word: the planting and the rooting of all that was done in the citadel of K'iche', in the nation of the K'iche' people.
:::

## Before creation

::: parallel 2
K'a katz'ininoq, K'a kachamamoq, Katz'inonik, K'a kasilanik, K'a kalolinik, Katolona' puch u pa kaj.
---
All is still silent, still at rest. It is silent, it is still motionless, still hushed; and empty is the womb of the sky.
:::

::: parallel 3
WA'E k'ut e nab'e tzij, Nab'e ucha'n. Maja b'i'oq jun winaq, Jun chikop, Tz'ikin, Kar, Tap, Che', Ab'aj, Jul, Siwan, K'im, K'eche'laj.
---
THESE, then, are the first words, the first speech. There is not yet one person, not one animal, bird, fish, crab, tree, stone, hollow, canyon, meadow or forest.
:::

::: parallel 4
TA xpe k'ut u tzij waral, Xul kuk' Ri Tepew, Q'ukumatz, Waral chi q'equ'mal, Chi aq'ab'al.
---
THEN came his word here. It came to Tepew and Q'ukumatz, here in the darkness, in the night.
:::

::: note Words worth knowing
- ***tikib'a'***, "to plant": the authors do not say they will merely record the old word but that they will *plant* it, as maize is planted, so that it grows again. *Xe'* is "root".
- ***Tepew***: "Sovereign", a title of majesty; ***Q'ukumatz***: "Quetzal Serpent", the K'iche' counterpart of the Yucatec Kukulkan and the Nahua Quetzalcoatl. In the following lines they talk with the Heart of Sky, Juraqan (the source of the word "hurricane"), and the world comes into being through their speech: "Earth!", they say, and it rises from the water.
- ***u pa kaj***, "the womb (literally 'the inside, belly') of the sky": the image of the sky as a body holding what is yet to be born.
:::
