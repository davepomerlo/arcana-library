::: note About this text
The *Huainanzi* ("Masters of Huainan") is a compendium in 21 chapters produced by scholars at the court of Liu An, King of Huainan, and presented to his nephew Emperor Wu in 139 BCE as a guide to everything a ruler should know. It draws heavily on the *Laozi* and *Zhuangzi*. Liu An was later accused of rebellion and killed himself in 122 BCE; Daoist legend says instead that he became an immortal and rose to heaven with his household, his dogs and chickens following.

Chapter 3, *Tianwen* ("Heavenly Patterns"), is a treatise on astronomy and the calendar. It opens with this cosmogony, one of the most influential in East Asia. Almost a thousand years later, Japan's official history, the *Nihon shoki* (720 CE), copied several of its phrases word for word for its own account of creation. See [the opening of the Nihon shoki](nihon-shoki-opening.html).

The whole work was translated into English for the first time only in 2010, by a team led by John Major.
:::

## Before heaven and earth

::: parallel 1
天墬未形，馮馮翼翼，洞洞灟灟，故曰太昭。
道始于虛霩，虛霩生宇宙，宇宙生氣。
---
When heaven and earth had not yet taken shape, all was vast and formless, a deep and fluid void: this is called the Great Inception.
The Way began in empty expanse; empty expanse gave birth to space and time; space and time gave birth to qi.
:::

## Heaven, earth, sun and moon

::: parallel 2
氣有涯垠，清陽者薄靡而為天，重濁者凝滯而為地。
清妙之合專易，重濁之凝竭難，故天先成而地後定。
---
Qi had boundaries. What was clear and light drifted up and became heaven; what was heavy and turbid congealed and became earth.
The clear and subtle unite easily; the heavy and turbid harden with difficulty. So heaven was completed first and earth was settled afterwards.
:::

::: parallel 3
天地之襲精為陰陽，陰陽之專精為四時，四時之散精為萬物。
積陽之熱氣生火，火氣之精者為日；積陰之寒氣為水，水氣之精者為月；日月之淫為精者為星辰。
---
The joined essences of heaven and earth became yin and yang; the concentrated essences of yin and yang became the four seasons; the scattered essences of the four seasons became the ten thousand things.
The hot qi of accumulated yang produced fire, and the essence of fire's qi became the sun. The cold qi of accumulated yin became water, and the essence of water's qi became the moon. The overflowing essence of sun and moon became the stars and planets.
:::

::: note Words worth knowing
- **太昭 *taizhao***, "Great Inception" (some editions read 太始 *taishi*, "Great Beginning"): the name for the state before anything was distinct.
- **宇宙 *yuzhou***: in this text *yu* is the extent of space and *zhou* the extent of time. It is now the ordinary Chinese and Japanese word for "universe" (*uchū* in Japanese).
- **氣 *qi***: breath, vapour, the energy-stuff of which everything is made. The Taiping yulan quotation reads 元氣 *yuanqi*, "primordial qi".
- **The four seasons** stand for time itself: once there is a yearly cycle, things can be born, grow and die.
:::
