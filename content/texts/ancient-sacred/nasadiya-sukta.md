::: parallel 1
नासदासीन्नो सदासीत्तदानीं नासीद्रजो नो व्योमा परो यत् ।
किमावरीवः कुह कस्य शर्मन्नम्भः किमासीद्गहनं गभीरम् ॥
---
nāsad āsīn no sad āsīt tadānīṃ nāsīd rajo no vyomā paro yat |
kim āvarīvaḥ kuha kasya śarmann ambhaḥ kim āsīd gahanaṃ gabhīram ||
---
There was neither non-being nor being then; there was no air, nor the sky beyond it.
What stirred? Where? In whose keeping? Was there water, unfathomably deep?
:::

::: parallel 2
न मृत्युरासीदमृतं न तर्हि न रात्र्या अह्न आसीत्प्रकेतः ।
आनीदवातं स्वधया तदेकं तस्माद्धान्यन्न परः किं चनास ॥
---
na mṛtyur āsīd amṛtaṃ na tarhi na rātryā ahna āsīt praketaḥ |
ānīd avātaṃ svadhayā tad ekaṃ tasmād dhānyan na paraḥ kiṃ canāsa ||
---
There was no death then, nor deathlessness; there was no sign to tell night from day.
That One breathed, windless, by its own impulse. Other than that there was nothing at all.
:::

::: parallel 3
तम आसीत्तमसा गूळ्हमग्रेऽप्रकेतं सलिलं सर्वमा इदम् ।
तुच्छ्येनाभ्वपिहितं यदासीत्तपसस्तन्महिनाजायतैकम् ॥
---
tama āsīt tamasā gūḷham agre 'praketaṃ salilaṃ sarvam ā idam |
tucchyenābhv apihitaṃ yad āsīt tapasas tan mahinājāyataikam ||
---
Darkness was hidden by darkness in the beginning; all this was water without a sign.
What came into being, covered by the void, that One was born by the power of heat (*tapas*).
:::

::: parallel 4
कामस्तदग्रे समवर्तताधि मनसो रेतः प्रथमं यदासीत् ।
सतो बन्धुमसति निरविन्दन्हृदि प्रतीष्या कवयो मनीषा ॥
---
kāmas tad agre sam avartatādhi manaso retaḥ prathamaṃ yad āsīt |
sato bandhum asati nir avindan hṛdi pratīṣyā kavayo manīṣā ||
---
Desire (*kāma*) came upon it in the beginning, which was the first seed of mind.
Searching in their hearts with wisdom, the poets found the bond of being in non-being.
:::

::: parallel 5
तिरश्चीनो विततो रश्मिरेषामधः स्विदासी३दुपरि स्विदासी३त् ।
रेतोधा आसन्महिमान आसन्स्वधा अवस्तात्प्रयतिः परस्तात् ॥
---
tiraścīno vitato raśmir eṣām adhaḥ svid āsī3d upari svid āsī3t |
retodhā āsan mahimāna āsan svadhā avastāt prayatiḥ parastāt ||
---
Their cord was stretched across. Was there a below? Was there an above?
There were seed-placers, there were powers: impulse below, offering above.
:::

::: parallel 6
को अद्धा वेद क इह प्र वोचत्कुत आजाता कुत इयं विसृष्टिः ।
अर्वाग्देवा अस्य विसर्जनेनाथा को वेद यत आबभूव ॥
---
ko addhā veda ka iha pra vocat kuta ājātā kuta iyaṃ visṛṣṭiḥ |
arvāg devā asya visarjanenāthā ko veda yata ābabhūva ||
---
Who really knows? Who here will declare it? Where was it born from, where does this creation come from?
The gods came later, with the creation of this world. Who, then, knows where it arose?
:::

::: parallel 7
इयं विसृष्टिर्यत आबभूव यदि वा दधे यदि वा न ।
यो अस्याध्यक्षः परमे व्योमन्सो अङ्ग वेद यदि वा न वेद ॥
---
iyaṃ visṛṣṭir yata ābabhūva yadi vā dadhe yadi vā na |
yo asyādhyakṣaḥ parame vyoman so aṅga veda yadi vā na veda ||
---
Where this creation arose, whether it made itself or whether it did not,
the one who watches over it in the highest heaven surely knows. Or perhaps he does not know.
:::

::: note Translation notes
**सत् / असत् *sat* / *asat*** are “being” and “non-being”, literally “the existent” and “the non-existent”. The hymn begins *before* that pair.

**तपस् *tapas*** is heat: the heat of brooding, of ascetic effort, of incubation. **स्वधा *svadhā*** is a thing's own power or innate impulse.

The closing line, “or perhaps he does not know”, is famous for its uncertainty. Some commentators read it as rhetorical, implying that the overseer certainly knows.
:::
