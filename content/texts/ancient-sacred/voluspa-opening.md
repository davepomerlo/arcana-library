::: parallel 1
Hljóðs bið ek allar
helgar kindir,
meiri ok minni
mögu Heimdallar;
viltu at ek, Valföðr,
vel fyr telja
forn spjöll fira,
þau er fremst of man.
---
I ask for a hearing from all
the holy races,
greater and lesser,
the children of Heimdall.
You wish, Father of the Slain,
that I should tell well
the ancient tales of the living,
the earliest I remember.
:::

::: parallel 2
Ek man jötna
ár of borna,
þá er forðum mik
fædda höfðu;
níu man ek heima,
níu íviðjur,
mjötvið mæran
fyr mold neðan.
---
I remember giants
born at the dawn of time,
who long ago
had brought me up.
I remember nine worlds,
nine giantesses of the wood,
the glorious tree of measure
beneath the earth.
:::

::: parallel 3
Ár var alda,
þat er ekki var,
vara sandr né sær
né svalar unnir;
jörð fannsk æva
né upphiminn,
gap var ginnunga,
en gras hvergi.
---
It was the dawn of ages,
when nothing was:
there was no sand nor sea
nor cool waves;
earth was nowhere to be found,
nor heaven above;
there was a yawning gap, Ginnungagap,
and grass nowhere.
:::

::: parallel 4
Áðr Burs synir
bjöðum of yppðu,
þeir er Miðgarð
mæran skópu;
sól skein sunnan
á salar steina,
þá var grund gróin
grænum lauki.
---
Then the sons of Bur
lifted up the lands,
they who shaped
glorious Midgard.
The sun shone from the south
on the stones of the hall,
and the ground was grown over
with green leeks.
:::

::: parallel 5
Sól varp sunnan,
sinni mána,
hendi inni hægri
um himinjöður;
sól þat né vissi
hvar hon sali átti,
stjörnur þat né vissu
hvar þær staði áttu,
máni þat né vissi
hvat hann megins átti.
---
The sun, companion of the moon,
cast from the south
her right hand
over the rim of heaven.
The sun did not know
where she had her halls;
the stars did not know
where they had their places;
the moon did not know
what power he had.
:::

::: parallel 6
Þá gengu regin öll
á rökstóla,
ginnheilög goð,
ok um þat gættusk;
nótt ok niðjum
nöfn of gáfu,
morgin hétu
ok miðjan dag,
undorn ok aftan,
árum at telja.
---
Then all the powers went
to their seats of judgement,
the most holy gods,
and took counsel together.
To night and her children
they gave names;
they named morning
and midday,
afternoon and evening,
to reckon the years.
:::

::: note Translation notes
**Valföðr**, “Father of the Slain”, is Odin, who has asked the seeress to speak. **mögu Heimdallar**, “children of Heimdall”, means all humankind. In the Eddic poem *Rígsþula*, Heimdall fathers the social classes.

**mjötviðr** is literally “measure-tree” or “tree of fate”, probably Yggdrasil while still a seed beneath the earth.

**Ginnungagap** is usually glossed “the yawning gap”. The first element, *ginn-*, may also mean “magical” or “deceptive”, as in *ginnheilög* in stanza 6.
:::
