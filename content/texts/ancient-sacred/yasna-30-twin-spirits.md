::: note About this text
The five *Gāθās* ("songs") are the oldest part of the Avesta, the scripture of Zoroastrianism, and the only part traditionally attributed to Zarathustra himself. Their language, Old Avestan, is close to the Sanskrit of the Rigveda. The hymns were handed down orally, in a ritual setting, for well over a thousand years before a special alphabet was invented to write them down, probably in the 5th or 6th century CE.

Yasna 30 is the source of Zoroastrian dualism. It speaks of two primal spirits, one later called *Spǝṇta Mainiiu* ("the beneficent spirit") and the other *Aŋra Mainiiu* (Middle Persian *Ahriman*, "the hostile spirit"). Later Zoroastrian theology and the Zurvanite heresy read the word "twins" literally and gave them a father, Time (*Zurvan*). Modern scholars disagree on whether the Gathas describe a cosmic battle, an ethical choice in each person, or (as Jean Kellens argues) the stages of the fire ritual.
:::

## Yasna 30.3

::: parallel 1
at̰ tā mainiiū pauruiiē, yā yǝ̄mā xvafǝnā asruuātǝm
manahicā vacahicā, šiiaoθanōi hī vahiiō akǝmcā
åscā hudåŋhō, ǝrǝš vīšiiātā nōit̰ duždåŋhō
---
Now these two spirits, in the beginning, the twins who were heard of in a dream:
in thought, in word and in deed they are the better and the bad.
And between these two, those who give well have chosen rightly, not those who give badly.
:::

## Yasna 30.4

::: parallel 2
at̰cā hiiat̰ tā hǝ̄m mainiiū, jasaētǝm paouruuīm dazdē
gaēmcā ajiiāitīmcā, yaθācā aŋhat̰ apǝ̄mǝm aŋhuš
acištō drǝguuatąm, at̰ aṣ̌āunē vahištǝm manō
---
And when these two spirits first came together, they established life and non-life,
and how existence shall be at the last:
the worst for the followers of the Lie, but for the follower of Truth the best thought.
:::

::: note Words worth knowing
- ***mainiiu*** (later *mainyu*): "spirit, mentality, inspiration", related to Sanskrit *manyu* and to *manah* "mind" in the same stanza.
- ***yǝ̄mā***: "twins", the same word as Sanskrit *yama*, the name of the first mortal in the Rigveda, who has a twin sister Yamī. ***xvafǝnā***: usually "in sleep, in a dream"; some translators read "by their own nature" instead.
- ***aṣ̌a*** and ***druj***: Truth or right order, and the Lie. The *aṣ̌auuan* is the one who follows Truth, the *drǝguuant* the follower of the Lie. *Aṣ̌a* corresponds to Vedic *ṛta*.
- ***gaēm ajiiāitīm***: "life and non-life". The choice the spirits make sets up the whole later history of the world, which will end with the triumph of Truth.
:::
