::: note About this text
The book known in the West as *The Tibetan Book of the Dead* is part of a large cycle of "peaceful and wrathful deities" (*zhi khro*) that the 14th-century treasure-finder Karma Lingpa said he discovered hidden in a mountain, where Padmasambhava had concealed it centuries before. Its best-known part is read aloud to the dying and the dead: *Bar do thos grol*, "liberation through hearing in the intermediate state".

At its centre stand six short verses, one for each *bardo* (བར་དོ་), "in-between state":

1. the bardo of this life (*skye gnas*);
2. the bardo of dream (*rmi lam*);
3. the bardo of meditation (*bsam gtan*);
4. the bardo of dying (*'chi kha*);
5. the bardo of reality itself (*chos nyid*);
6. the bardo of becoming (*srid pa*), which leads to rebirth.

As Bryan Cuevas has shown, the text was little known in Tibet until the 1927 English version by W. Y. Evans-Wentz and Kazi Dawa Samdup made it famous abroad. Its Western fame is out of all proportion to its standing at home.
:::

## The bardo of this life

::: parallel 1
ཀྱཻ་མ༔ བདག་ལ་སྐྱེ་གནས་བར་དོ་འཆར་དུས་འདིར༔
ཚེ་ལ་ལོངས་མེད་ལེ་ལོ་སྤངས་བྱས་ནས༔
ཐོས་བསམ་སྒོམ་གསུམ་མ་ཡེངས་ལམ་ལ་འཇུག༔
སྣང་སེམས་ལམ་བསློང་སྐུ་གསུམ་མངོན་གྱུར་བྱ༔
མི་ལུས་ལན་གཅིག་ཐོབ་པའི་དུས་ཚོད་འདིར༔
ཡེངས་མ་ལམ་ལ་སྡོད་པའི་དུས་ཚོད་མིན༔
---
kyé ma, dak la kyé né bar do char dü dir
tsé la long mé lelo pang ché né
tö sam gom sum ma yeng lam la juk
nang sem lam long ku sum ngön gyur cha
mi lü len chik tobpé dü tsö dir
yeng ma lam la döpé dü tsö min
---
Alas! Now, as the bardo of this life dawns upon me,
life has no time to spare: I will give up laziness
and enter, undistracted, the path of hearing, reflecting and meditating.
Taking appearances and mind as the path, I will make the three kāyas real.
Now, at this one time that I have gained a human body,
there is no time to sit idly on the path of distraction.
:::

## The bardo of dream

::: parallel 2
ཀྱཻ་མ༔ བདག་ལ་རྨི་ལམ་བར་དོ་འཆར་དུས་འདིར༔
གཏི་མུག་རོ་ཉལ་བག་མེད་སྤངས་བྱས་ནས༔
དྲན་པ་ཡེངས་མེད་གནས་ལུགས་ངང་ལ་འཇོག༔
རྨི་ལམ་བཟུང་ནས་སྤྲུལ་བསྒྱུར་འོད་གསལ་སྦྱངས༔
དུད་འགྲོ་བཞིན་དུ་ཉལ་བར་མ་བྱེད་ཅིག༔
གཉིད་དང་མངོན་སུམ་འདྲེས་པའི་ཉམས་ལེན་གཅེས༔
---
kyé ma, dak la milam bardo char dü dir
timuk ro nyel bakmé pang ché né
drenpa yeng mé neluk ngang la jok
milam zung né trül gyur ösel jang
düdro zhin du nyelwar ma ché chik
nyi dang ngönsum drepé nyamlen ché
---
Alas! Now, as the bardo of dream dawns upon me,
I will give up the careless, corpse-like sleep of ignorance
and let mindfulness rest, undistracted, in the natural state.
Holding the dream, I will train in emanating and transforming, and in clear light.
Do not sleep like an animal!
Cherish the practice that mingles sleep with direct experience.
:::

::: note Words worth knowing
- **kyé ma** ཀྱཻ་མ: an exclamation of sorrow or urgency, "alas!"
- **thos bsam sgom gsum** ཐོས་བསམ་སྒོམ་གསུམ: the three stages of learning: hearing the teaching, reflecting on it, meditating on it.
- **sku gsum** སྐུ་གསུམ: the three bodies (*kāya*) of a buddha: the dharma body, the enjoyment body and the emanation body.
- **'od gsal** འོད་གསལ: "clear light", the luminous nature of mind, which the dream practices aim to recognise in sleep.
- The tsheg-mark **༔** (*ter tsheg*) at the end of each line marks this as a *terma*, a revealed treasure text.
:::
