## The visionary: Swedenborg's *Earths in the Universe* (1758)

The Swedish scientist and mystic **Emanuel Swedenborg** wrote that his inner senses had been opened so that he could speak with spirits and angels — not only those of this earth but those of other worlds, sometimes for a day, sometimes for weeks or months. In *Earths in the Universe* he describes in turn the spirits and inhabitants of Mercury, Jupiter, Mars, Saturn, Venus and the Moon, and of five earths "in the starry heaven" beyond the solar system. His argument for their existence is partly astronomical (the starry heaven is so immense that it would be absurd for it to be empty) and partly theological (the universe exists so that there can be a human race, and so a heaven). The planets he lists are the ones known in his day; Uranus, discovered in 1781, is absent.

*Read it:* [sacred-texts.com](https://sacred-texts.com/swd/eiu/eiu00.htm). More of Swedenborg's works are listed in the [sacred-texts catalogue](../sacred-texts.html#st-swedenborg).

## The medium: Hélène Smith and the Martian language (1894–1900)

"Hélène Smith" was the pseudonym of **Catherine-Elise Müller** (1861–1929), a Geneva medium. In trance, guided by a spirit called Leopold, she relived lives as a fifteenth-century Indian princess and as Marie Antoinette, and from 1894 she travelled in spirit to Mars, describing its landscapes and people. From 1896 she spoke and wrote a Martian language with its own script.

The psychologist **Théodore Flournoy** (1854–1920) of the University of Geneva sat in on her séances for years and published *Des Indes à la planète Mars* (1899; English translation 1900). He was sympathetic but sceptical. He traced the Martian romance to what Hélène's "subliminal imagination" had absorbed — in particular, talk in the spiritualist circles she frequented about whether Mars, with its newly reported "canals", was inhabited — and he showed the Martian texts were in effect a disguised form of French. His book made her famous; she refused to work with him again. It is a founding text of the psychology of mediumship and of the idea of **cryptomnesia**: forgotten memories returning as apparently new revelations.

*Read it:* [sacred-texts.com](https://www.sacred-texts.com/ufo/ipm/index.htm).

## The saucer books (1950–1956)

Two books from the start of the flying-saucer era show the two directions the subject took.

- **Donald Keyhoe, *The Flying Saucers Are Real* (1950).** A former Marine aviator and Naval Academy graduate, Keyhoe was commissioned by *True* magazine in 1949 to investigate the reports. He concluded that the saucers were probably interplanetary and that the Air Force was hiding what it knew. The book was a bestseller and set the "cover-up" theme for decades. *[Read it](https://sacred-texts.com/ufo/fsar/fsar00.htm).*
- **Edward J. Ruppelt, *The Report on Unidentified Flying Objects* (1956).** Ruppelt had headed the Air Force's own investigation (Project Blue Book) in the early 1950s. His book is an insider's account: critical of Air Force muddle and secrecy, but far more cautious about what the reports proved. *[Read it](https://neonvagabond.xyz/mirrors/sacred-texts/ufo/rufo/rufo00.htm).*

## The contactees (1950s)

In the 1950s a group of writers in the United States went further than Keyhoe: they said they had *met* the occupants of the saucers, who were usually beautiful, human-looking "Space Brothers" from Venus, Mars or Saturn bringing warnings about atomic weapons and messages of cosmic love. Their books, several of them at sacred-texts.com, include **Orfeo Angelucci**'s *The Secret of the Saucers* (1955) and **George Hunt Williamson**'s *Other Tongues — Other Flesh*, which mixes saucer lore with lost continents and esoteric history.

Investigators found no physical evidence for any of these meetings, and the messages closely reflect the hopes and fears of their time: the Bomb, the Cold War, and the spiritualist and Theosophical ideas many contactees already held. The way reports change with the times — airships in the 1890s, saucers in the 1950s, triangles and drone-like lights today — is one reason the historian David Clarke, who advised The National Archives on the British files, calls UFOs "very much a social phenomenon".

## Channelled sources

From the 1950s onward, some channellers said their sources were not spirits of the dead but extraterrestrial or "higher-density" beings. The best-documented example in this archive is the **Ra Material** (1981–84), sessions recorded by L/L Research in Kentucky, whose source described itself as a "social memory complex" once associated with Venus. See the [Ra Material case file](ra-material-law-of-one.html), the [guide to channelled literature](channelled-literature-guide.html), and the [L/L Research tracker](../channelling-tracker.html).

## Ancient astronauts (1968 onward)

In 1968 the Swiss hotel manager **Erich von Däniken** published *Erinnerungen an die Zukunft* (in English *Chariots of the Gods?*), arguing that visitors from space had come to Earth in antiquity, taught early civilisations and been remembered as gods. He pointed to the pyramids, the Nazca lines in Peru and the carved sarcophagus lid of the Maya king Pakal at Palenque, which he read as an astronaut at the controls of a rocket. The book became a worldwide bestseller.

Archaeologists rejected it almost unanimously. An analysis in the Penn Museum's *Expedition* magazine shows that the argument rests on "it looks like a spaceman to me": Pakal's lid, read in the context of Classic Maya art, shows the king's death, not a flight. Critics have also pointed out that the theory consistently credits outsiders with the achievements of non-European peoples, and that the monuments he cites are explained by the known skills and tools of the people who built them.

## What the cases have in common

- **Contact happens in private states or private meetings** — visions, trance, solitary encounters — and leaves nothing others can examine.
- **The aliens reflect their era.** Swedenborg's planet-dwellers worship the Lord as he did; Hélène Smith's Mars has the canals of the 1890s; the contactees' Venusians worry about the Bomb.
- **The best investigators were not debunkers.** Flournoy took Hélène Smith seriously as a person and as a psychological phenomenon. His approach — to ask what the experience reveals about the mind and the culture, rather than only whether it is true — remains the most useful way to read these texts.
