::: note What "channelled" means here
In this archive, *channelled* covers any text whose producers said it came from a source other than themselves: spirits of the dead, angels, "higher selves" or extraterrestrial groups. The methods vary: trance speech, automatic writing, the planchette and Ouija board, or a conscious "inner voice". Listing a text here records what its makers claimed. It does not endorse the claim.
:::

## How the genre developed

**Spiritualism (from 1848).** After the Fox sisters' rappings at Hydesville, New York, séances spread across America and Europe. Most were about contacting the dead, but some mediums produced long teachings. Andrew Jackson Davis had already dictated *The Principles of Nature* (1847) in what he called a magnetic trance.

**Spiritism (from 1857).** In Paris, Allan Kardec turned séance answers into a systematic philosophy in *Le Livre des Esprits*. Its question-and-answer form became the model for later channelled books. [Read questions 1–11 in the archive.](kardec-spirits-book-on-god.html)

**Psychical research (from 1882).** The Society for Psychical Research began testing mediums. Its most famous subject, the Boston trance medium **Leonora Piper**, was studied for decades, and Richard Hodgson's long report on her (1898) is still a key document for anyone weighing such claims.

**Literary mediumship (1913–1937).** Pearl Curran of St Louis produced novels and thousands of poems on a Ouija board as **"Patience Worth"**, a supposed seventeenth-century Englishwoman. W. B. Yeats built *A Vision* (1925; revised 1937) from his wife George's automatic writing.

**The New Age (from the 1960s).** Channelled books became a genre of their own:

- **The Seth Material** (from 1963): Jane Roberts in trance, with her husband Robert Butts taking dictation.
- **A Course in Miracles** (1976): Helen Schucman's inner dictation, taken down 1965–1972.
- **The Ra Material / The Law of One** (1981–1984): Carla Rueckert in trance, questioned by Don Elkins. [Case file.](ra-material-law-of-one.html)

**The Urantia Book** (1955) stands slightly apart. Its 196 papers were said to come through an unnamed sleeping subject in Chicago, and its exact origins are still debated.

## Questions worth asking of any channelled text

1. **Who asked the questions?** In question-and-answer material, the questioner's interests often shape the content as much as the source does.
2. **What was edited?** Compare the published books with the session records where they survive. Seth, Ra and *A Course in Miracles* all had editors.
3. **What did the medium already know?** Check the content against the books and ideas the medium and sitters had read.
4. **Is anything checkable?** Statements about history, science or the future can be tested. Most of these texts make few such claims, and the claims they do make have rarely held up.
