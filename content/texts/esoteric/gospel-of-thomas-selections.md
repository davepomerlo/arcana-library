::: note About this text
In December 1945, farmers digging near the cliffs of Jabal al-Ṭārif, by Nag Hammadi in Upper Egypt, found a jar holding thirteen leather-bound papyrus books. Among them was a complete Coptic text of a "gospel" made up only of sayings of Jesus, 114 of them by modern count, with no story of his birth, miracles or death.

Scholars soon realised that three Greek papyrus fragments found at Oxyrhynchus around 1900 were parts of the same work. How old it is remains disputed. Some date an early core of sayings to the 1st century; others place the collection in the 2nd century, after the canonical gospels. About half the sayings have parallels in Matthew, Mark and Luke; others are found nowhere else.

The gospel is often called Gnostic, but it does not set out the myth of a flawed creator found in other Nag Hammadi texts. Its focus is on finding the meaning of the sayings, knowing oneself, and discovering a "kingdom" that is already present.
:::

## The prologue and saying 1

ⲛⲁⲉⲓ ⲛⲉ ⲛ̄ϣⲁϫⲉ ⲉⲑⲏⲡ ⲉⲛⲧⲁⲓ̄ⲥ̄ ⲉⲧⲟⲛϩ ϫⲟⲟⲩ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲁϥⲥϩⲁⲓⲥⲟⲩ ⲛ̄ϭⲓ ⲇⲓⲇⲩⲙⲟⲥ ⲓⲟⲩⲇⲁⲥ ⲑⲱⲙⲁⲥ.
ⲁⲩⲱ ⲡⲉϫⲁϥ ϫⲉ ⲡⲉⲧⲁϩⲉ ⲉⲑⲉⲣⲙⲏⲛⲉⲓⲁ ⲛ̄ⲛⲉⲉⲓϣⲁϫⲉ ϥⲛⲁϫⲓ ϯⲡⲉ ⲁⲛ ⲙ̄ⲡⲙⲟⲩ.
---
These are the hidden sayings that the living Jesus spoke, and that Didymos Judas Thomas wrote down.
And he said: "Whoever finds the interpretation of these sayings will not taste death."
:::

ⲁⲩⲱ ϩⲟⲧⲁⲛ ⲉϥϣⲁⲛϭⲓⲛⲉ ϥⲛⲁϣⲧⲣ̄ⲧⲣ̄
ⲁⲩⲱ ⲉϥϣⲁⲛⲧⲟⲣ̄ⲧⲣ̄ ϥⲛⲁⲣ̄ϣⲡⲏⲣⲉ
ⲁⲩⲱ ϥⲛⲁⲣ̄ⲣ̄ⲣⲟ ⲉϫⲙ̄ ⲡⲧⲏⲣϥ.
---
Jesus said: "Let the one who seeks not stop seeking until he finds.
And when he finds, he will be troubled;
and when he is troubled, he will marvel;
and he will reign over the All."
:::

## Saying 77

::: parallel 77
ⲡⲉϫⲉ ⲓ̄ⲥ̄ ϫⲉ ⲁⲛⲟⲕ ⲡⲉ ⲡⲟⲩⲟⲉⲓⲛ ⲡⲁⲉⲓ ⲉⲧϩⲓϫⲱⲟⲩ ⲧⲏⲣⲟⲩ.
ⲁⲛⲟⲕ ⲡⲉ ⲡⲧⲏⲣϥ· ⲛ̄ⲧⲁ ⲡⲧⲏⲣϥ ⲉⲓ ⲉⲃⲟⲗ ⲛ̄ϩⲏⲧ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲛ̄ⲧⲁ ⲡⲧⲏⲣϥ ⲡⲱϩ ϣⲁⲣⲟⲉⲓ.
ⲡⲱϩ ⲛ̄ⲛⲟⲩϣⲉ ⲁⲛⲟⲕ ϯⲙ̄ⲙⲁⲩ·
ϥⲓ ⲙ̄ⲡⲱⲛⲉ ⲉϩⲣⲁⲓ ⲁⲩⲱ ⲧⲉⲧⲛⲁϩⲉ ⲉⲣⲟⲉⲓ ⲙ̄ⲙⲁⲩ.
---
Jesus said: "I am the light that is over them all.
I am the All: the All came forth from me, and the All reached as far as me.
Split a piece of wood, and I am there.
Lift up the stone, and you will find me there."
:::

::: note Notes on the text
- **Saying 2.** The Greek fragment (P. Oxy. 654) adds a further step: "and having reigned, he will rest." The Coptic ends with reigning over "the All" (ⲡⲧⲏⲣϥ, *ptērf*), a word used in Gnostic texts for the whole of divine reality.
- **Saying 77.** In the Greek papyrus P. Oxy. 1 the words about the wood and the stone come at the end of a different saying (30). The Coptic has moved them here, perhaps because of the wordplay on ⲡⲱϩ, which means both "to split" and "to reach".
- **ⲓ̄ⲥ̄** is the usual abbreviation for the name Jesus, marked with a stroke above.
:::
