::: note Why this text matters
The *Neiye* 內業 was overlooked for a long time because it sits in the *Guanzi*, a miscellany named after a statesman of the 7th century BCE. Harold Roth's work in the 1990s showed that it is a self-contained meditation manual. It describes a practice of aligning the body, stilling the mind and refining *qi*, which later Daoist inner alchemy built on.
:::

::: parallel 1
凡物之精，此則為生。
下生五穀，上為列星。
流於天地之間，謂之鬼神；
藏於胸中，謂之聖人。
---
The vital essence of all things: this is what brings them to life.
Below, it gives birth to the five grains; above, it becomes the arrayed stars.
When it flows between heaven and earth, we call it ghosts and spirits;
when it is stored within the breast, we call that person a sage.
:::

::: parallel 2
是故此氣，杲乎如登於天，杳乎如入於淵，
淖乎如在於海，卒乎如在於屺。
是故此氣也，不可止以力，而可安以德；
不可呼以聲，而可迎以意。
敬守勿失，是謂成德。
德成而智出，萬物畢得。
---
So this *qi*: bright, as if climbing into the sky; dark, as if entering an abyss;
vast, as if in the ocean; sudden, as if on a mountain peak.
So this *qi* cannot be held by force, yet it can be settled by virtue;
it cannot be summoned by the voice, yet it can be welcomed by intent.
Guard it reverently and do not lose it: this is called perfecting virtue.
When virtue is perfected, wisdom emerges, and all things are fully grasped.
:::

::: parallel 3
凡心之刑，自充自盈，自生自成。
其所以失之，必以憂樂喜怒欲利。
能去憂樂喜怒欲利，心乃反濟。
彼心之情，利安以寧，勿煩勿亂，和乃自成。
---
The form of the mind fills itself and overflows of itself; it is born of itself and completes itself.
Where it is lost, it is always through sorrow, pleasure, joy, anger, desire and profit.
If you can cast off sorrow, pleasure, joy, anger, desire and profit, the mind returns to its balance.
The true condition of the mind is that it benefits from calm and stillness. Do not trouble it, do not disturb it, and harmony will form by itself.
:::

::: parallel 19
摶氣如神，萬物備存。
能摶乎？能一乎？
能無卜筮而知吉凶乎？
能止乎？能已乎？
能勿求諸人而得之己乎？
思之思之，又重思之。
思之而不通，鬼神將通之。
非鬼神之力也，精氣之極也。
---
Concentrate your *qi* as if it were spirit, and all things will be present within you.
Can you concentrate? Can you be one?
Can you know good and bad fortune without divination by shell and stalk?
Can you stop? Can you be still?
Can you find it in yourself without seeking it from others?
Think about it, think about it, and think about it again.
If you think and still do not get through, the ghosts and spirits will get you through.
But it is not the power of ghosts and spirits: it is the utmost of vital essence and *qi*.
:::

::: note Translation notes
**精 *jīng*** is “vital essence”: the finest, most concentrated form of *qi*. **氣 *qì*** is the breath-energy that makes up everything. **刑 *xíng*** in verse 3 stands for 形, “form”.

**Verse 19** is an early statement of the claim, central to later Daoism, that inner cultivation can replace outer divination. The tortoise shell (卜) and yarrow stalks (筮) of the *Yijing* tradition become unnecessary.
:::
