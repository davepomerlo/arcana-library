::: note About this text
The *Haṭhapradīpikā* ("light on haṭha"), compiled by Svātmārāma in the 15th century, gathered verses from earlier works into the most influential manual of haṭha yoga. Its four chapters cover postures, breath control and cleansing practices, seals and locks (*mudrā*, *bandha*), and absorption in sound. James Mallinson and Jason Birch have traced many of its verses to older texts, at least one of them, the *Amṛtasiddhi*, Buddhist, and a team at SOAS, Oxford and Marburg has produced the first critical edition.

In haṭha yoga, breath is *prāṇa* or *vāyu*, "wind", the life force itself. To hold it is to hold life; to master it is to master the mind. The verses below state the theory in its simplest form.
:::

## Why the breath is restrained

::: parallel 2.1
अथासने दृढे योगी वशी हितमिताशनः ।
गुरूपदिष्टमार्गेण प्राणायामान्समभ्यसेत् ॥
---
athāsane dṛḍhe yogī vaśī hitamitāśanaḥ |
gurūpadiṣṭamārgeṇa prāṇāyāmān samabhyaset ||
---
Now, once posture is firm, the yogi, self-controlled and eating wholesome food in moderation,
should practise the breath controls in the way the guru has taught.
:::

::: parallel 2.2
चले वाते चलं चित्तं निश्चले निश्चलं भवेत् ।
योगी स्थाणुत्वमाप्नोति ततो वायुं निरोधयेत् ॥
---
cale vāte calaṃ cittaṃ niścale niścalaṃ bhavet |
yogī sthāṇutvam āpnoti tato vāyuṃ nirodhayet ||
---
When the breath moves, the mind moves; when the breath is still, the mind becomes still.
The yogi attains the steadiness of a pillar. Therefore one should restrain the breath.
:::

::: parallel 2.3
यावद्वायुः स्थितो देहे तावज्जीवनमुच्यते ।
मरणं तस्य निष्क्रान्तिस्ततो वायुं निरोधयेत् ॥
---
yāvad vāyuḥ sthito dehe tāvaj jīvanam ucyate |
maraṇaṃ tasya niṣkrāntis tato vāyuṃ nirodhayet ||
---
As long as the breath stays in the body, that is called life.
Death is its departure. Therefore one should restrain the breath.
:::

## The warning

::: parallel 2.15
यथा सिंहो गजो व्याघ्रो भवेद्वश्यः शनैः शनैः ।
तथैव सेवितो वायुरन्यथा हन्ति साधकम् ॥
---
yathā siṃho gajo vyāghro bhaved vaśyaḥ śanaiḥ śanaiḥ |
tathaiva sevito vāyur anyathā hanti sādhakam ||
---
As a lion, an elephant or a tiger is tamed slowly, little by little,
so the breath must be handled; otherwise it kills the practitioner.
:::

::: parallel 2.16
प्राणायामेन युक्तेन सर्वरोगक्षयो भवेत् ।
अयुक्ताभ्यासयोगेन सर्वरोगसमुद्गमः ॥
---
prāṇāyāmena yuktena sarvarogakṣayo bhavet |
ayuktābhyāsayogena sarvarogasamudgamaḥ ||
---
By proper breath control all diseases are destroyed;
by improper practice all diseases arise.
:::

::: note Breath control and modern research
The claim of 2.2, that slowing the breath calms the mind, is one that laboratory research partly supports. A 2018 systematic review from the University of Pisa found that slow breathing (under ten breaths a minute) raises heart-rate variability and alpha brain waves and is linked with greater relaxation and less anxiety. But it could include only 15 studies, and it says nothing about long breath retention, which the *Haṭhapradīpikā* goes on to teach.

The text's own warning in 2.15–16 deserves to be taken seriously. Breath-holding and forceful breathing can cause fainting, and should never be practised in or near water or while driving. People with heart or lung conditions, or who are pregnant, should ask a doctor first. See the archive's guide to [breath and meditation research](breath-meditation-research.html).
:::
