::: note About this text
The *Qingjing jing* is a Tang-dynasty scripture that recasts the Daodejing in the language of Buddhist emptiness. Its method is a triple contemplation: look inward at the mind, outward at the body, and far off at things, and find that none of them has a self of its own. Seeing that, the mind stops reaching and settles by itself. Later Inner Alchemy (*neidan* 內丹) teachers, and the Quanzhen order to this day, treated it as a core text for practice.
:::

## The Great Dao

::: parallel 1
老君曰：
大道無形，生育天地；
大道無情，運行日月；
大道無名，長養萬物；
吾不知其名，強名曰道。
---
Lord Lao said:
The Great Dao has no form, yet it gives birth to Heaven and Earth.
The Great Dao has no feelings, yet it moves the sun and moon on their courses.
The Great Dao has no name, yet it raises and nourishes the ten thousand things.
I do not know its name; forced to name it, I call it the Dao.
:::

::: parallel 2
夫道者：有清有濁，有動有靜；
天清地濁，天動地靜；
男清女濁，男動女靜。
降本流末，而生萬物。
清者濁之源，動者靜之基。
人能常清靜，天地悉皆歸。
---
The Dao has the clear and the turbid, movement and stillness.
Heaven is clear and Earth is turbid; Heaven moves and Earth is still.
The male is clear and the female turbid; the male moves and the female is still.
Descending from the root and flowing out to the branches, it gives birth to the ten thousand things.
The clear is the source of the turbid; movement is the foundation of stillness.
If a person can be constantly clear and still, Heaven and Earth will all return to them.
:::

## Why the mind is not still

::: parallel 3
夫人神好清，而心擾之；
人心好靜，而欲牽之。
常能遣其欲，而心自靜；
澄其心，而神自清。
自然六欲不生，三毒消滅。
所以不能者，為心未澄，欲未遣也。
---
The human spirit loves clarity, but the mind disturbs it.
The human mind loves stillness, but desires drag it away.
If you can always send desires away, the mind becomes still of itself.
Let the mind settle like water, and the spirit becomes clear of itself.
Then, quite naturally, the six desires do not arise and the three poisons melt away.
Those who cannot do this have not let the mind settle and have not sent desire away.
:::

## The triple contemplation

::: parallel 4
能遣之者，
內觀其心，心無其心；
外觀其形，形無其形；
遠觀其物，物無其物。
三者既悟，唯見於空。
---
For those who can send desire away:
Look inward at the mind, and the mind has no mind of its own.
Look outward at the body, and the body has no body of its own.
Look far off at things, and things have no things of their own.
Once these three are understood, you see only emptiness.
:::

::: parallel 5
觀空亦空，空無所空；
所空既無，無無亦無；
無無既無，湛然常寂。
寂無所寂，欲豈能生？
欲既不生，即是真靜。
真常應物，真常得性；
常應常靜，常清靜矣。
---
Contemplate emptiness, and emptiness too is empty; emptiness has nothing to empty.
Since there is nothing to be emptied, even “nothing” is nothing.
Once “nothing of nothing” is gone, there is deep and constant quiet.
When quiet has nothing left to be quiet about, how could desire arise?
When desire no longer arises, that is true stillness.
The true and constant responds to things; the true and constant attains its nature.
Constantly responding, constantly still: this is constant clarity and stillness.
:::

::: parallel 6
如此清靜，漸入真道；
既入真道，名為得道。
雖名得道，實無所得；
為化眾生，名為得道；
能悟之者，可傳聖道。
---
Clear and still in this way, you gradually enter the true Dao.
Having entered the true Dao, it is called “attaining the Dao”.
Though it is called attaining the Dao, in truth nothing is attained.
Only to teach living beings is it called attaining the Dao.
Whoever can awaken to this may pass on the sacred Dao.
:::

## On contention and the false mind

::: parallel 7
老君曰：
上士無爭，下士好爭；
上德不德，下德執德。
執著之者，不名道德。
---
Lord Lao said:
The highest adept does not contend; the lowest adept loves to contend.
Highest virtue is not virtuous; lowest virtue clings to virtue.
Those who cling and grasp cannot be said to have the Dao or its virtue.
:::

::: parallel 8
眾生所以不得真道者，為有妄心。
既有妄心，即驚其神；
既驚其神，即著萬物；
既著萬物，即生貪求；
既生貪求，即是煩惱。
煩惱妄想，憂苦身心。
便遭濁辱，流浪生死，
常沉苦海，永失真道。
---
Living beings do not attain the true Dao because they have a deluded mind.
Having a deluded mind, they startle the spirit.
Having startled the spirit, they attach to the ten thousand things.
Having attached to things, they give rise to craving.
Having given rise to craving, that is affliction.
Affliction and deluded thinking bring sorrow and pain to body and mind.
So they meet with defilement and disgrace, drift through birth and death,
sink forever in the sea of suffering, and lose the true Dao for good.
:::

::: parallel 9
真常之道，悟者自得。
得悟之者，常清靜矣。
---
The true and constant Dao: those who awaken attain it by themselves.
Those who attain awakening are constantly clear and still.
:::

## Colophons of the transmitters

::: parallel 10
仙人葛玄曰：
吾得真道，嘗誦此經萬遍。
此經是天人所習，不傳下士。
吾昔受之於東華帝君，
東華帝君受之於金闕帝君，
金闕帝君受之於西王母，
皆口口相傳，不記文字。
吾今於世，書而錄之。
上士悟之，昇為天官；
中士悟之，南宮列仙；
下士悟之，在世長年。
遊行三界，昇入金門。
---
The Immortal Ge Xuan said:
I attained the true Dao, and I have recited this scripture ten thousand times.
This scripture is studied by the beings of Heaven and is not passed on to lesser adepts.
Long ago I received it from the Imperial Lord of Eastern Florescence (Donghua Dijun);
he received it from the Imperial Lord of the Golden Gate (Jinque Dijun);
he received it from the Queen Mother of the West (Xiwangmu).
All of them passed it on by word of mouth and did not write it down.
Now, in this world, I have written it down.
Higher adepts who awaken to it ascend to become officials of Heaven;
middling adepts who awaken to it join the immortals of the Southern Palace;
lesser adepts who awaken to it live long in the world.
They roam the Three Realms and ascend through the Golden Gate.
:::

::: parallel 11
左玄真人曰：
學道之士，持誦此經萬遍，
十天善神，衛護其人，
玉符保身，金液鍊形，
形神俱妙，與道合真。
---
The Perfected of the Left Mystery said:
If a student of the Dao keeps and recites this scripture ten thousand times,
the good spirits of the Ten Heavens will guard that person;
jade talismans will protect the body, and golden elixir will refine the form,
until body and spirit are both made wondrous and unite with the Dao in truth.
:::

::: parallel 12
正一真人曰：
人家有此經，悟解之者，
災障不干，眾聖護門，
神昇上界，朝拜高尊。
功滿德就，相感帝君。
誦持不退，身騰紫雲。
---
The Perfected of Orthodox Unity said:
In a household that has this scripture, whoever awakens to its meaning
will not be troubled by calamity or obstruction; the sages will guard the gate,
the spirit will rise to the upper realm and pay court to the High Venerable.
When merit is full and virtue complete, the Imperial Lord will respond.
Keep reciting without falling back, and the body will rise on purple clouds.
:::

::: note Translation notes
**清靜 qīngjìng** is translated “clear and still” (often “purity and tranquillity”). 清 is the clarity of water once the silt has settled, and 澄 *chéng* in section 3 is the verb for that settling.

**六欲 six desires and 三毒 three poisons.** These are Buddhist terms. The three poisons are greed, anger and delusion.

**上士、中士、下士** are “higher, middling and lesser adepts”, a ranking borrowed from Daodejing ch. 41.
:::
