::: note About the text
No Greek original has been found. The earliest known version is Arabic, in the *Kitāb sirr al-khalīqa* (“Book of the Secret of Creation”), a work attributed to Balīnās, the Arabic name for Apollonius of Tyana. Latin translations reached Europe in the twelfth and thirteenth centuries, most influentially through the *Secretum secretorum*. The Latin “vulgate” below is the version alchemists actually used. Translators number its lines in different ways.
:::

::: parallel 1
Verum, sine mendacio, certum et verissimum.
---
True, without falsehood, certain and most true.
:::

::: parallel 2
Quod est inferius est sicut quod est superius, et quod est superius est sicut quod est inferius, ad perpetranda miracula rei unius.
---
What is below is like what is above, and what is above is like what is below, for accomplishing the miracles of the one thing.
:::

::: parallel 3
Et sicut omnes res fuerunt ab uno, meditatione unius, sic omnes res natae fuerunt ab hac una re, adaptatione.
---
And as all things came from the One, by the meditation of the One, so all things were born from this one thing, by adaptation.
:::

::: parallel 4
Pater eius est Sol, mater eius Luna; portavit illud ventus in ventre suo; nutrix eius terra est.
---
Its father is the Sun, its mother the Moon; the wind carried it in its belly; its nurse is the earth.
:::

::: parallel 5
Pater omnis telesmi totius mundi est hic.
---
This is the father of every perfection (*telesma*) in the whole world.
:::

::: parallel 6
Vis eius integra est, si versa fuerit in terram.
---
Its power is complete if it is turned into earth.
:::

::: parallel 7
Separabis terram ab igne, subtile ab spisso, suaviter, cum magno ingenio.
---
You shall separate the earth from the fire, the subtle from the dense, gently and with great skill.
:::

::: parallel 8
Ascendit a terra in coelum, iterumque descendit in terram, et recipit vim superiorum et inferiorum.
---
It rises from earth to heaven, and descends again to earth, and receives the power of the higher and the lower things.
:::

::: parallel 9
Sic habebis gloriam totius mundi. Ideo fugiet a te omnis obscuritas.
---
So you will have the glory of the whole world, and all darkness will flee from you.
:::

::: parallel 10
Hic est totius fortitudinis fortitudo fortis, quia vincet omnem rem subtilem, omnemque solidam penetrabit.
---
This is the strong strength of all strength, for it will overcome every subtle thing and penetrate every solid one.
:::

::: parallel 11
Sic mundus creatus est.
---
So the world was created.
:::

::: parallel 12
Hinc erunt adaptationes mirabiles, quarum modus est hic.
---
From this will come wonderful adaptations, of which this is the method.
:::

::: parallel 13
Itaque vocatus sum Hermes Trismegistus, habens tres partes philosophiae totius mundi.
---
Therefore I am called Hermes Trismegistus, having the three parts of the philosophy of the whole world.
:::

::: parallel 14
Completum est quod dixi de operatione Solis.
---
What I have said about the operation of the Sun is complete.
:::

::: note Translation notes
**“As above, so below”** is a modern summary of line 2, not a phrase in the text. The Latin compares rather than equates: *est sicut*, “is like”.

**telesmum** (line 5) comes from the Greek *telesma*, “completion” or “consecrated object”, which is also the root of “talisman”. Some scholars think this word shows that the tablet began as a text of talismanic magic rather than alchemy.

**The three parts of philosophy** (line 13) were read in the Middle Ages as the mineral, vegetable and animal realms, or as alchemy, astrology and theurgy. The name *Trismegistus* means “thrice-greatest”.
:::
