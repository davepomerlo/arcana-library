::: note About the poem
The *Masnavi-ye Ma'navi* (“Rhyming Couplets of Inner Meaning”) runs to some 25,000 couplets in six books. Rumi dictated it to his disciple Ḥusām al-Dīn Chalabī. The opening eighteen couplets are said to be the only ones Rumi wrote down himself. The *ney*, the reed flute, became the emblem of the Mevlevi order, whose ceremony (*samāʿ*) begins with its sound.
:::

::: parallel 1
بشنو این نی چون شکایت می‌کند
از جدایی‌ها حکایت می‌کند
---
Listen to this reed as it complains,
telling a tale of separations:
:::

::: parallel 2
کز نیستان تا مرا ببریده‌اند
در نفیرم مرد و زن نالیده‌اند
---
“Ever since they cut me from the reed-bed,
men and women have wept at my cry.
:::

::: parallel 3
سینه خواهم شرحه شرحه از فراق
تا بگویم شرح درد اشتیاق
---
I want a breast torn to shreds by separation,
so that I can tell it the pain of longing.
:::

::: parallel 4
هر کسی کاو دور ماند از اصل خویش
باز جوید روزگار وصل خویش
---
Everyone who is left far from their source
seeks again the time of their union.
:::

::: parallel 5
من به هر جمعیتی نالان شدم
جفت بدحالان و خوش‌حالان شدم
---
In every gathering I have lamented;
I have kept company with the sad and the happy.
:::

::: parallel 6
هر کسی از ظن خود شد یار من
از درون من نجست اسرار من
---
Each became my friend from their own opinion;
none sought out my secrets from within me.
:::

::: parallel 7
سر من از نالهٔ من دور نیست
لیک چشم و گوش را آن نور نیست
---
My secret is not far from my lament,
but eye and ear do not have that light.
:::

::: parallel 8
تن ز جان و جان ز تن مستور نیست
لیک کس را دید جان دستور نیست
---
Body is not veiled from soul, nor soul from body,
yet no one is permitted to see the soul.”
:::

::: parallel 9
آتش است این بانگ نای و نیست باد
هر که این آتش ندارد نیست باد
---
This cry of the reed is fire, not wind:
whoever does not have this fire, may they be nothing!
:::

::: parallel 10
آتش عشق است کاندر نی فتاد
جوشش عشق است کاندر می فتاد
---
It is the fire of love that has fallen into the reed;
it is the ferment of love that has fallen into the wine.
:::

::: parallel 11
نی حریف هر که از یاری برید
پرده‌هایش پرده‌های ما درید
---
The reed is the companion of all who have been cut off from a friend;
its melodies (*pardeh*) have torn away our veils (*pardeh*).
:::

::: parallel 12
همچو نی زهری و تریاقی که دید
همچو نی دمساز و مشتاقی که دید
---
Who ever saw a poison and an antidote like the reed?
Who ever saw a soul-companion and a lover like the reed?
:::

::: parallel 13
نی حدیث راه پر خون می‌کند
قصه‌های عشق مجنون می‌کند
---
The reed tells of the road full of blood;
it tells the love stories of Majnun.
:::

::: parallel 14
محرم این هوش جز بیهوش نیست
مر زبان را مشتری جز گوش نیست
---
None is intimate with this sense but the senseless;
the tongue has no customer but the ear.
:::

::: parallel 15
در غم ما روزها بیگاه شد
روزها با سوزها همراه شد
---
In our grief the days have grown late;
the days have travelled hand in hand with burning.
:::

::: parallel 16
روزها گر رفت گو رو باک نیست
تو بمان ای آن که چون تو پاک نیست
---
If the days are gone, say: “Go, it does not matter.”
But you remain, O you, than whom none is purer.
:::

::: parallel 17
هر که جز ماهی ز آبش سیر شد
هر که بی روزیست روزش دیر شد
---
Everyone except the fish has had their fill of His water;
whoever lacks daily bread finds the day long.
:::

::: parallel 18
در نیابد حال پخته هیچ خام
پس سخن کوتاه باید والسلام
---
The raw cannot grasp the state of the ripe;
so the word must be short. And so, farewell.
:::

::: note Translation notes
**The puns.** Persian *pardeh* means both a musical mode or fret and a veil or curtain (couplet 11). In couplet 9, *nīst bād* is used twice: “is not wind” and “may it be nothing”.

**Majnun** is the lover of Layla in the Arabic and Persian romance, driven mad (*majnūn*) by love. In Sufi poetry he stands for the lover of God.

**The raw and the ripe** (*khām* and *pokhteh*, literally “uncooked” and “cooked”) is Rumi's favourite image for spiritual maturity.
:::
