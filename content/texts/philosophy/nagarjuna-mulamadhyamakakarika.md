::: note Key terms
***pratītyasamutpāda*** is “dependent origination”: everything arises in dependence on conditions. ***śūnyatā*** is “emptiness”: being empty of *svabhāva*, an intrinsic, independent nature. ***prapañca*** is the proliferation of concepts that overlays experience.
:::

## Dedicatory verses

::: parallel 1
anirodham anutpādam anucchedam aśāśvatam |
anekārtham anānārtham anāgamam anirgamam ||
yaḥ pratītyasamutpādaṃ prapañcopaśamaṃ śivam |
deśayāmāsa saṃbuddhas taṃ vande vadatāṃ varam ||
---
No cessation and no arising, no annihilation and no permanence,
no identity and no difference, no coming and no going:
the fully awakened one taught this dependent origination,
the peaceful stilling of conceptual proliferation. I bow to him, the best of speakers.
:::

## The nature of things (chapter 18)

::: parallel 18.9
aparapratyayaṃ śāntaṃ prapañcair aprapañcitam |
nirvikalpam anānārtham etat tattvasya lakṣaṇam ||
---
Not dependent on another, peaceful, not elaborated by conceptual proliferation,
free of conceptual construction, without diversity: this is the mark of reality.
:::

## The two truths (chapter 24)

::: parallel 24.8
dve satye samupāśritya buddhānāṃ dharmadeśanā |
lokasaṃvṛtisatyaṃ ca satyaṃ ca paramārthataḥ ||
---
The Buddhas' teaching of the Dharma rests on two truths:
the conventional truth of the world, and the truth in the ultimate sense.
:::

::: parallel 24.9
ye 'nayor na vijānanti vibhāgaṃ satyayor dvayoḥ |
te tattvaṃ na vijānanti gambhīraṃ buddhaśāsane ||
---
Those who do not understand the distinction between these two truths
do not understand the deep reality in the Buddha's teaching.
:::

::: parallel 24.10
vyavahāram anāśritya paramārtho na deśyate |
paramārtham anāgamya nirvāṇaṃ nādhigamyate ||
---
Without relying on everyday practice, the ultimate cannot be taught;
without reaching the ultimate, nirvāṇa is not attained.
:::

::: parallel 24.18
yaḥ pratītyasamutpādaḥ śūnyatāṃ tāṃ pracakṣmahe |
sā prajñaptir upādāya pratipat saiva madhyamā ||
---
Dependent origination is what we call emptiness.
That is a dependent designation, and that itself is the middle way.
:::

::: parallel 24.19
apratītya samutpanno dharmaḥ kaścin na vidyate |
yasmāt tasmād aśūnyo hi dharmaḥ kaścin na vidyate ||
---
Since there is no thing at all that has not arisen dependently,
there is no thing at all that is not empty.
:::

## Nirvāṇa (chapter 25)

::: parallel 25.19
na saṃsārasya nirvāṇāt kiṃcid asti viśeṣaṇam |
na nirvāṇasya saṃsārāt kiṃcid asti viśeṣaṇam ||
---
There is nothing whatever that distinguishes saṃsāra from nirvāṇa;
there is nothing whatever that distinguishes nirvāṇa from saṃsāra.
:::

::: parallel 25.20
nirvāṇasya ca yā koṭiḥ koṭiḥ saṃsaraṇasya ca |
na tayor antaraṃ kiṃcit susūkṣmam api vidyate ||
---
The limit of nirvāṇa is the limit of saṃsāra;
between the two there is not even the slightest gap.
:::

::: parallel 25.24
sarvopalambhopaśamaḥ prapañcopaśamaḥ śivaḥ |
na kva cit kasyacit kaścid dharmo buddhena deśitaḥ ||
---
Peace is the stilling of all grasping, the stilling of conceptual proliferation.
No Dharma was ever taught by the Buddha to anyone, anywhere.
:::

::: note Translation notes
**24.18** is the most debated verse in Madhyamaka. In “that is a dependent designation (*prajñaptir upādāya*)”, “that” may refer to emptiness itself. Emptiness, too, would then be only a conventional label and not a hidden absolute.

**25.24** completes the argument. If everything is empty, then the teaching is empty as well. Its liberating power works within convention, not by pointing to an ultimate thing.
:::
