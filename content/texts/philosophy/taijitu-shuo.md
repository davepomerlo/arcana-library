::: note About this text
Zhou Dunyi drew a diagram of five tiers: an empty circle, a circle of interlocking light and dark bands, the five phases joined by lines, and two more circles for the "way of Qian" and "way of Kun" and for the birth of all things. This short text explains it. The diagram itself seems to come from Daoist sources: Song scholars traced it to the Daoist Chen Tuan 陳摶, and Qing scholars to diagrams linked with the alchemical *Cantong qi*.

The first four words, 無極而太極, caused one of the great debates of Chinese philosophy. Zhu Xi read them as "ultimateless, and yet the supreme ultimate": two sides of one principle. Lu Jiuyuan and his brother objected that *wuji* is a Daoist word taken from the Daodejing (chapter 28) and that Confucius had spoken only of *taiji*. A variant reading recorded in Song times, 自無極而為太極 "from the Ultimateless there came to be the Supreme Ultimate", would make the sequence a process in time, which Zhu Xi rejected.
:::

## From the Ultimateless to the ten thousand things

::: parallel 1
無極而太極。
太極動而生陽，動極而靜，靜而生陰，靜極復動。
一動一靜，互為其根；分陰分陽，兩儀立焉。
---
The Ultimateless, and yet the Supreme Ultimate.
The Supreme Ultimate moves and gives rise to yang. Movement at its limit becomes stillness; stillness gives rise to yin. Stillness at its limit returns to movement.
Now moving, now still, each is the root of the other. Yin and yang divide, and the Two Modes are established.
:::

::: parallel 2
陽變陰合，而生水火木金土。五氣順布，四時行焉。
五行，一陰陽也；陰陽，一太極也；太極，本無極也。
五行之生也，各一其性。
---
Yang transforms and yin unites, and they give rise to water, fire, wood, metal and earth. These five *qi* spread out in due order, and the four seasons run their course.
The five phases are one yin and yang; yin and yang are one Supreme Ultimate; the Supreme Ultimate is at root the Ultimateless.
As the five phases arise, each has its own nature.
:::

::: parallel 3
無極之真，二五之精，妙合而凝。
乾道成男，坤道成女，二氣交感，化生萬物。
萬物生生，而變化無窮焉。
---
The reality of the Ultimateless and the essence of the Two and the Five unite mysteriously and take shape.
The way of Qian becomes the male; the way of Kun becomes the female. The two *qi* stir and respond to each other, and by transformation give birth to the ten thousand things.
The ten thousand things produce and reproduce, and their changes have no end.
:::

## The human being and the sage

::: parallel 4
惟人也，得其秀而最靈。形既生矣，神發知矣。
五性感動，而善惡分，萬事出矣。
聖人定之以中正仁義而主靜，立人極焉。
---
Human beings alone receive the finest of it and are the most spiritually aware. Once the body is formed, the spirit awakens to knowing.
The five natures are stirred into action, good and evil are distinguished, and the myriad affairs arise.
The sage settles them by the mean, correctness, humaneness and rightness, and makes stillness the ruling principle. So he establishes the ultimate standard for humankind.
:::

::: parallel 5
故聖人與天地合其德，日月合其明，四時合其序，鬼神合其吉凶。
君子修之吉，小人悖之凶。
---
So the sage is one with Heaven and Earth in virtue, with the sun and moon in brightness, with the four seasons in order, and with the ghosts and spirits in good and ill fortune.
The noble person who cultivates this has good fortune; the petty person who goes against it has misfortune.
:::

::: parallel 6
故曰：「立天之道，曰陰與陽；立地之道，曰柔與剛；立人之道，曰仁與義。」
又曰：「原始反終，故知死生之說。」
大哉易也，斯其至矣！
---
So it is said: "The way of Heaven is established as yin and yang; the way of Earth as soft and hard; the way of humankind as humaneness and rightness."
And again: "Trace things to their beginning and follow them back to their end, and so you understand the meaning of death and life."
Great is the *Changes*! Here it reaches its height.
:::

::: note Textual notes
- The two closing quotations are from the *Shuogua* and *Xici* commentaries to the Yijing. Section 5 also quotes the *Wenyan* commentary.
- In section 2, the Gutenberg text of the *Jinsi lu* reads 各一其行 for 各一其性, and in section 3 二五之真 for 二五之精. Both are obvious slips; all the other witnesses read 性 and 精.
- Some editions print Zhou's own short notes beside the text, for example 無欲故靜 "without desire, hence still" beside 主靜. They are left out here.
:::
