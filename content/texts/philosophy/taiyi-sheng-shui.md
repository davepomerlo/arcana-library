::: note About this text
In 1993, after tomb robbers had broken into a small tomb at Guodian in Hubei, archaeologists recovered about 800 inscribed bamboo slips, buried around 300 BCE. They included the oldest known versions of parts of the *Daodejing* and a dozen texts nobody had seen for more than two thousand years. One of them, written on 14 slips bound with the *Laozi* material, opens with the words 大一生水, "the Great One gave birth to water". The editors named it after them.

It is one of the very few Chinese cosmogonies to begin with water, and its method is unusual: each thing produced turns back and *helps* (輔 *fu*) what produced it, and the pair together give rise to the next stage. Scholars disagree whether it is Daoist, a work of the "yin-yang" school, or rooted in astronomy, where *Taiyi* was the name of the pole star and its god. The Taiwanese scholar Chen Ligui notes that its later part, "the Way of heaven values weakness", is hard to separate from Daoist thought.

The full Guodian corpus was translated into English for the first time by Scott Cook, *The Bamboo Texts of Guodian* (2012).
:::

## The generation of the world

::: parallel 1
大一生水，水反輔大一，是以成天。
天反輔大一，是以成地。
天地【復相輔】也，是以成神明。
神明復相輔也，是以成陰陽。
陰陽復相輔也，是以成四時。
四時復【相】輔也，是以成滄熱。
滄熱復相輔也，是以成濕燥。
濕燥復相輔也，成歲而止。
---
The Great One gave birth to water. Water turned back and helped the Great One, and so heaven was formed.
Heaven turned back and helped the Great One, and so earth was formed.
Heaven and earth [helped each other in turn], and so the spirits above and below were formed.
The spirits helped each other in turn, and so yin and yang were formed.
Yin and yang helped each other in turn, and so the four seasons were formed.
The four seasons helped each other in turn, and so cold and heat were formed.
Cold and heat helped each other in turn, and so moisture and dryness were formed.
Moisture and dryness helped each other in turn; the year was formed, and there it stopped.
:::

## The way back

::: parallel 2
故歲者，濕燥之所生也。濕燥者，滄熱之所生也。滄熱者，四時之所生也。四時者，陰陽之所生【也】。陰陽者，神明之所生也。神明者，天地之所生也。天地者，大一之所生也。
---
So the year is what moisture and dryness produced. Moisture and dryness are what cold and heat produced. Cold and heat are what the four seasons produced. The four seasons are what yin and yang produced. Yin and yang are what the spirits produced. The spirits are what heaven and earth produced. Heaven and earth are what the Great One produced.
:::

## The Great One hidden in water

::: parallel 3
是故大一藏於水，行於時，周而又【始】，□□□萬物母。
一缺一盈，以己為萬物經。
此天之所不能殺，地之所不能埋，陰陽之所不能成。
---
Therefore the Great One is stored in water and moves with the seasons. It goes round and [begins] again, …… the mother of the ten thousand things.
Now waning, now waxing, it makes itself the warp-thread of the ten thousand things.
This is what heaven cannot destroy, what earth cannot bury, and what yin and yang cannot form.
:::

## Heaven is short in the north-west

::: parallel 4
天地名字並立，故過其方，不思相【尚】。
【天不足】於西北，其下高以強。
地不足於東南，其上□□□。
天道貴弱，削成者以益生者。
---
Heaven and earth each have their name and style, standing side by side; so though each goes beyond its own domain, they do not think to [outdo] one another.
[Heaven is lacking] in the north-west, and what lies below it there is high and strong.
Earth is lacking in the south-east, and what lies above it there is ……
The Way of heaven values weakness: it cuts back what is complete to add to what is growing.
:::

::: note Notes
- **神明 *shenming*** is hard to translate: "the spirits", "the divine and the bright", or the powers of heaven and earth. Wen Xing and Henricks render it "the gods above and below".
- **Heaven lacking in the north-west.** This reflects a myth known from the *Huainanzi* and other texts: in a battle, the giant Gonggong broke the pillar holding up the north-western sky, so heaven tilts and the stars move west, while the earth sinks in the south-east and the rivers flow east.
- **Water first.** The *Daodejing* praises water as the closest thing to the Way (ch. 8, 78). Some scholars see this text as working out that idea as a cosmology.
:::
