## 1 · The butterfly dream (from chapter 2, “On Seeing Things as Equal” 齊物論)

::: parallel 2
昔者莊周夢為胡蝶，栩栩然胡蝶也，自喻適志與！不知周也。
俄然覺，則蘧蘧然周也。
不知周之夢為胡蝶與，胡蝶之夢為周與？
周與胡蝶，則必有分矣。此之謂物化。
---
Once Zhuang Zhou dreamed he was a butterfly, a butterfly fluttering happily about, enjoying itself and doing as it pleased. It did not know it was Zhou.
Suddenly he woke, and there he was, solidly Zhou.
He did not know whether he was Zhou who had dreamed he was a butterfly, or a butterfly dreaming it was Zhou.
Between Zhou and the butterfly there must be some distinction. This is called the transformation of things.
:::

## 2 · Cook Ding carves an ox (chapter 3, “The Essentials of Nurturing Life” 養生主)

::: parallel 3a
庖丁為文惠君解牛，手之所觸，肩之所倚，足之所履，膝之所踦，砉然嚮然，奏刀騞然，莫不中音。合於桑林之舞，乃中經首之會。
文惠君曰：「譆，善哉！技蓋至此乎？」
---
Cook Ding was carving an ox for Lord Wenhui. Wherever his hand touched, his shoulder leaned, his foot stepped or his knee pressed, there was a ripping sound, a whooshing sound, and the blade sang as it went, every stroke in rhythm. It matched the Mulberry Grove dance and kept time with the Jingshou music.
Lord Wenhui said: “Ah, wonderful! Can skill really reach such heights?”
:::

::: parallel 3b
庖丁釋刀對曰：「臣之所好者道也，進乎技矣。
始臣之解牛之時，所見無非牛者。三年之後，未嘗見全牛也。
方今之時，臣以神遇而不以目視，官知止而神欲行。
依乎天理，批大郤，導大窾，因其固然，技經肯綮之未嘗，而況大軱乎！」
---
Cook Ding put down his knife and replied: “What your servant cares about is the Dao, which goes beyond skill.
When I first began carving oxen, all I could see was the ox. After three years I no longer saw the whole ox.
Now I meet it with my spirit and do not look with my eyes. My senses know when to stop, and my spirit moves as it wishes.
I follow the natural pattern (*tianli*), strike at the big gaps, guide the blade through the big hollows, and go along with how the ox is actually made. I never touch even a ligament or tendon, let alone a big bone!”
:::

::: parallel 3c
「良庖歲更刀，割也；族庖月更刀，折也。
今臣之刀十九年矣，所解數千牛矣，而刀刃若新發於硎。
彼節者有間，而刀刃者無厚；以無厚入有間，恢恢乎其於遊刃必有餘地矣，是以十九年而刀刃若新發於硎。」
---
“A good cook changes his knife once a year, because he cuts. An ordinary cook changes his knife once a month, because he hacks.
I have had this knife for nineteen years and carved thousands of oxen with it, yet its edge is as if fresh from the whetstone.
The joints have spaces between them, and the edge of the blade has no thickness. When what has no thickness enters where there is space, there is plenty of room for the blade to play. That is why after nineteen years the edge is as if fresh from the whetstone.”
:::

::: parallel 3d
「雖然，每至於族，吾見其難為，怵然為戒，視為止，行為遲，動刀甚微，謋然已解，如土委地。
提刀而立，為之四顧，為之躊躇滿志，善刀而藏之。」
文惠君曰：「善哉！吾聞庖丁之言，得養生焉。」
---
“Even so, whenever I come to a knotty place and see that it will be hard, I take care and go slowly. My gaze settles, my movements slow down, I move the blade very gently, and suddenly it falls apart, like a clod of earth crumbling to the ground.
I stand with the knife in my hand, look all around, linger there content, then wipe the blade and put it away.”
Lord Wenhui said: “Wonderful! From hearing Cook Ding's words I have learned how to nurture life.”
:::

## 3 · The fasting of the mind (chapter 4, “In the Human World” 人間世)

::: parallel 4
回曰：「敢問心齋。」
仲尼曰：「若一志，無聽之以耳而聽之以心，無聽之以心而聽之以氣。
聽止於耳，心止於符。氣也者，虛而待物者也。
唯道集虛。虛者，心齋也。」
---
Yan Hui said: “May I ask what the fasting of the mind is?”
Confucius said: “Unify your will. Do not listen with your ears but with your mind. Do not listen with your mind but with your *qi*.
Listening stops at the ears; the mind stops at what matches it. *Qi* is empty and waits on things.
The Dao gathers only in emptiness. Emptiness is the fasting of the mind.”
:::

## 4 · The death of Hundun (end of chapter 7, “Responding to Emperors and Kings” 應帝王)

::: parallel 5
南海之帝為儵，北海之帝為忽，中央之帝為渾沌。
儵與忽時相與遇於渾沌之地，渾沌待之甚善。
儵與忽謀報渾沌之德，曰：「人皆有七竅，以視聽食息，此獨無有，嘗試鑿之。」
日鑿一竅，七日而渾沌死。
---
The emperor of the Southern Sea was Shu (Swift), the emperor of the Northern Sea was Hu (Sudden), and the emperor of the Centre was Hundun (Chaos).
From time to time Shu and Hu met in Hundun's land, and Hundun treated them very well.
Shu and Hu talked about how to repay Hundun's kindness. “All people have seven openings to see, hear, eat and breathe with. He alone has none. Let's try boring some for him.”
Each day they bored one opening. On the seventh day, Hundun died.
:::

## 5 · The joy of fish (end of chapter 17, “Autumn Floods” 秋水)

::: parallel 6
莊子與惠子遊於濠梁之上。
莊子曰：「儵魚出遊從容，是魚之樂也。」
惠子曰：「子非魚，安知魚之樂？」
莊子曰：「子非我，安知我不知魚之樂？」
惠子曰：「我非子，固不知子矣；子固非魚也，子之不知魚之樂，全矣。」
莊子曰：「請循其本。子曰『汝安知魚樂』云者，既已知吾知之而問我，我知之濠上也。」
---
Zhuangzi and Huizi were strolling on the bridge over the Hao River.
Zhuangzi said: “The minnows swim about so freely. This is the joy of fish.”
Huizi said: “You are not a fish. How do you know the joy of fish?”
Zhuangzi said: “You are not me. How do you know I do not know the joy of fish?”
Huizi said: “I am not you, so certainly I do not know you. You are certainly not a fish, so the case is complete: you do not know the joy of fish.”
Zhuangzi said: “Let's go back to where we started. When you said ‘How (*an*) do you know the joy of fish?’ you already knew that I knew it, and asked me *how*. I know it from here, above the Hao.”
:::

## 6 · Drumming on a pot (chapter 18, “Perfect Happiness” 至樂)

::: parallel 7
莊子妻死，惠子弔之，莊子則方箕踞鼓盆而歌。
惠子曰：「與人居，長子老身，死不哭亦足矣，又鼓盆而歌，不亦甚乎！」
---
When Zhuangzi's wife died, Huizi came to offer condolences and found Zhuangzi squatting with his legs sprawled out, drumming on a pot and singing.
Huizi said: “You lived with her, she raised your children and grew old with you. Not weeping at her death would be bad enough, but drumming on a pot and singing: isn't that too much?”
:::

::: parallel 8
莊子曰：「不然。是其始死也，我獨何能無概然！
察其始而本無生，非徒無生也而本無形，非徒無形也而本無氣。
雜乎芒芴之間，變而有氣，氣變而有形，形變而有生，今又變而之死，是相與為春秋冬夏四時行也。
人且偃然寢於巨室，而我嗷嗷然隨而哭之，自以為不通乎命，故止也。」
---
Zhuangzi said: “Not so. When she first died, how could I alone not grieve?
But I looked back to her beginning: originally she had no life; not only no life, but no form; not only no form, but no *qi*.
Mixed in among the vague and indistinct, there was a change and there was *qi*; the *qi* changed and there was form; the form changed and there was life. Now there has been another change, and she has gone to death. It is like the procession of the four seasons, spring, autumn, winter and summer.
She is lying peacefully asleep in the great room. If I followed her with sobbing and wailing, it would show I did not understand destiny. So I stopped.”
:::

::: note Translation notes
**物化 *wùhuà***, “the transformation of things”, is the key term of the butterfly story. It names the constant turning of one thing into another, which makes fixed identities look provisional.

**The pun in the fish story.** 安 *ān* can mean either “how?” or “from where?”. Zhuangzi's last answer, “I know it from here, above the Hao”, plays on the second sense.
:::
