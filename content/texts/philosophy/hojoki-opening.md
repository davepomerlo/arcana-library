::: note About this text
Kamo no Chōmei was born into the family of hereditary priests of the Kamo Shrine in Kyoto but lost the succession, and with it his career. He lived through the Great Fire of 1177, the whirlwind of 1180, the move of the capital to Fukuhara, the Yōwa famine of 1181–82 and the Genryaku earthquake of 1185, and records them all in the *Hōjōki*. Around fifty he became a Buddhist monk and finally built a hut of one *jō* square (about three metres), which he could take apart and carry on two carts.

The opening below, on the river, the foam and the morning glory, is one of the best-known passages in Japanese literature. Its theme is *mujō* 無常: nothing in the world, neither people nor their houses, lasts.
:::

## The river

::: parallel 1
ゆく河の流れは絶えずして、しかももとの水にあらず。
淀みに浮かぶうたかたは、かつ消えかつ結びて、久しくとどまりたる例なし。
世の中にある人と栖と、またかくのごとし。
---
Yuku kawa no nagare wa taezu shite, shikamo moto no mizu ni arazu.
Yodomi ni ukabu utakata wa, katsu kie katsu musubite, hisashiku todomaritaru tameshi nashi.
Yo no naka ni aru hito to sumika to, mata kaku no gotoshi.
---
The flowing river never stops, and yet the water is never the same water.
The foam that floats on its pools now vanishes, now forms again, and none of it has ever lasted long.
So it is with the people of this world and their dwellings.
:::

## The houses of the capital

::: parallel 2
たましきの都のうちに、棟を並べ、甍を争へる、高き、卑しき人の住まひは、世々を経て尽きせぬものなれど、これをまことかと尋ぬれば、昔ありし家はまれなり。
或は去年焼けて、今年作れり。或は大家ほろびて小家となる。
住む人もこれに同じ。所も変はらず、人も多かれど、いにしへ見し人は、二、三十人が中に、わづかにひとりふたりなり。
朝に死に、夕に生まるるならひ、ただ水の泡にぞ似たりける。
---
Tamashiki no miyako no uchi ni, mune wo narabe, iraka wo arasoeru, takaki, iyashiki hito no sumai wa, yoyo wo hete tsukisenu mono naredo, kore wo makoto ka to tazunureba, mukashi arishi ie wa mare nari.
Arui wa kozo yakete, kotoshi tsukureri. Arui wa ōie horobite koie to naru.
Sumu hito mo kore ni onaji. Tokoro mo kawarazu, hito mo ōkaredo, inishie mishi hito wa, ni-sanjūnin ga naka ni, wazuka ni hitori futari nari.
Ashita ni shini, yūbe ni umaruru narai, tada mizu no awa ni zo nitarikeru.
---
In the jewel-paved capital the houses of high and low stand ridge by ridge, their roof tiles vying with one another, and it seems they last from age to age. But ask whether this is true, and you find that few of the houses of the past remain.
Some burned last year and were rebuilt this year. Some great houses have fallen and become small ones.
The people who live in them are the same. The places have not changed and there are still many people, but of those I knew long ago, only one or two in twenty or thirty are left.
Dying in the morning, born in the evening: it is just like the foam on the water.
:::

## The dew and the morning glory

::: parallel 3
知らず、生まれ死ぬる人、いづかたより来たりて、いづかたへか去る。
また知らず、仮の宿り、誰がためにか心を悩まし、何によりてか目を喜ばしむる。
その、主と栖と無常を争ふさま、いはば朝顔の露に異ならず。
或は露落ちて花残れり。残るといへども朝日に枯れぬ。
或は花しぼみて露なほ消えず。消えずといへども夕べを待つことなし。
---
Shirazu, umare shinuru hito, izukata yori kitarite, izukata e ka saru.
Mata shirazu, kari no yadori, ta ga tame ni ka kokoro wo nayamashi, nani ni yorite ka me wo yorokobashimuru.
Sono, aruji to sumika to mujō wo arasou sama, iwaba asagao no tsuyu ni kotonarazu.
Arui wa tsuyu ochite hana nokoreri. Nokoru to iedomo asahi ni karenu.
Arui wa hana shibomite tsuyu nao kiezu. Kiezu to iedomo yūbe wo matsu koto nashi.
---
I do not know where the people who are born and die come from, or where they go.
Nor do I know for whose sake they trouble their hearts over these lodgings of a night, or why they take such pleasure in them.
Master and house race each other into impermanence, like the dew on a morning glory.
Sometimes the dew falls and the flower remains; but though it remains, it withers in the morning sun.
Sometimes the flower fades and the dew is still there; but though it is still there, it does not last until evening.
:::

::: note Words worth knowing
- **utakata** うたかた: foam or bubbles on water, a standard image of the transient.
- **sumika** 栖: dwelling, nest. Chōmei pairs *hito* and *sumika*, people and their houses, throughout the work.
- **tamashiki** たましき: "laid with jewels", a fixed epithet for the capital.
- **kari no yadori** 仮の宿り: "a temporary lodging", meaning this world and also the body.
- **asagao** 朝顔: in Chōmei's day the word may have meant a morning-blooming flower in general, not only the morning glory.
:::
