::: note About this text
The *Yoga Sūtras* are 195 aphorisms in four chapters, so terse that they were always read with a commentary. Most scholars now hold that the sūtras and the oldest commentary, attributed to Vyāsa, were composed together as one work, the *Pātañjalayogaśāstra*, around 400 CE.

Patañjali's yoga is a discipline of the mind built on the dualist philosophy of Sāṃkhya: an unchanging witness, the *puruṣa* or "seer", is distinct from everything that changes, including thought. Posture and breath are steps toward meditation, not ends in themselves. As Mark Singleton, James Mallinson and others have shown, the physical yoga practised worldwide today comes from much later *haṭha* texts and from 20th-century innovations. See the [Haṭhapradīpikā on breath](hatha-pradipika-pranayama.html).
:::

## The definition of yoga

::: parallel 1.1
अथ योगानुशासनम्
---
atha yogānuśāsanam
---
Now, the teaching of yoga.
:::

::: parallel 1.2
योगश्चित्तवृत्तिनिरोधः
---
yogaś cittavṛttinirodhaḥ
---
Yoga is the stilling of the movements of the mind.
:::

::: parallel 1.3
तदा द्रष्टुः स्वरूपेऽवस्थानम्
---
tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe 'vasthānam
---
Then the seer abides in its own form.
:::

::: parallel 1.4
वृत्तिसारूप्यमितरत्र
---
vṛttisārūpyam itaratra
---
Otherwise, it takes on the form of the movements.
:::

## The eight limbs, posture and breath

::: parallel 2.29
यमनियमासनप्राणायामप्रत्याहारधारणाध्यानसमाधयोऽष्टावङ्गानि
---
yamaniyamāsanaprāṇāyāmapratyāhāradhāraṇādhyānasamādhayo 'ṣṭāv aṅgāni
---
Restraints, observances, posture, breath control, withdrawal of the senses, concentration, meditation and absorption are the eight limbs.
:::

::: parallel 2.46
स्थिरसुखमासनम्
---
sthirasukham āsanam
---
Posture is steady and comfortable.
:::

::: parallel 2.49
तस्मिन्सति श्वासप्रश्वासयोर्गतिविच्छेदः प्राणायामः
---
tasmin sati śvāsapraśvāsayor gativicchedaḥ prāṇāyāmaḥ
---
When that [posture] is achieved, breath control is the interruption of the flow of in-breath and out-breath.
:::

::: note Words worth knowing
- **citta** चित्त: the mind as a whole, including thinking, feeling and memory.
- **vṛtti** वृत्ति: "turning, movement": the five kinds of mental activity listed in 1.6 (valid knowledge, error, imagination, sleep and memory).
- **nirodha** निरोध: "restraint, stilling, cessation". Whether yoga means quieting thought or ending it altogether is debated by commentators.
- **draṣṭṛ** द्रष्टृ: "the seer", pure awareness, which only ever witnesses.
- **āsana** आसन: literally "seat". For Patañjali it means a stable sitting position for meditation, not the many postures of modern yoga.
:::
